# 1. Ensemble principle

The expedition cannot function as a single alien viewpoint. The boy changes because he encounters a small group whose ideas about humanity, duty, memory, danger and care are meaningfully different.

The crew should initially appear more unified than they really are. Their disagreements emerge not from melodramatic betrayal but from incompatible definitions of responsibility.

Across Season One, the crew moves from **observers of a vanished civilisation** to **participants in a living relationship they can no longer treat as neutral research**.

The core quartet is:

1. Expedition Leader
2. Young Scientist
3. Security Officer
4. Ship Intelligence

Final personal names remain **OPEN**.

# 2. Expedition Leader

## Character essence

**LOCKED IN FUNCTION**  
The mission commander: calm, restrained and deeply committed to procedure until the boy forces a choice between legitimate authority and moral responsibility.

## One-line definition

A leader who believes good systems protect people, then discovers that obedience can become a way of avoiding responsibility.

## External want

**LOCKED**  
Complete the mission, protect the crew and return with a truthful account of Earth.

## Internal need

**LOCKED**  
Accept that responsibility begins where procedure stops being morally sufficient.

## Core contradiction

The leader is genuinely ethical, but has spent a lifetime trusting institutions to define the boundaries of ethical action.

That makes the sealed mission especially painful. The leader is not exposed as secretly cruel. They are exposed as someone whose faith in process has allowed them not to ask enough questions.

## Fear

**RECOMMENDED**  
Making a personal decision that harms the crew because it felt emotionally right.

This fear explains the hesitation that wounds the boy in the back half of the season. The leader does care. The problem is that care has not yet become action.

## Gift

- composure under pressure
- ability to hold multiple interpretations without rushing to certainty
- willingness to admit error once evidence becomes undeniable
- protective authority that does not need spectacle

## Flaw

Turns uncertainty into delay.

The leader can name every ethical complexity in the room while still postponing the decision that matters.

## Behavioural language

- speaks less when pressure rises
- keeps posture unusually still while others move
- rarely touches objects without permission
- notices who has not spoken yet
- physically positions themself where every crew member remains visible
- when emotionally compromised, becomes even more formal

## Relationship with the boy

### Beginning
The boy is a protected discovery and potential survivor.

### Middle
The leader begins to treat his practical knowledge as mission-critical and his preferences as relevant.

### Crisis
The sealed-order revelation shows that the leader still thinks they have the right to decide what is safest for him.

### Resolution
The leader gives the boy meaningful decision-making power, then accepts the institutional consequences of backing that choice.

The relationship should gradually acquire parental texture without the leader explicitly claiming a parental role.

## Relationship with the young scientist

The scientist represents the emotional and interpretive freedom the leader once had before responsibility narrowed their choices.

The leader admires the scientist's instinct but worries that curiosity without discipline can endanger the crew.

By the finale, each has learned something from the other: the scientist gains accountability; the leader regains moral immediacy.

## Relationship with the security officer

They have strong mutual trust built from prior missions.

The security officer will challenge the leader privately long before challenging them publicly. This makes the eventual break with command feel consequential rather than impulsive.

## Relationship with ship intelligence

The intelligence preserves institutional truth more perfectly than the leader would sometimes like.

When the sealed orders surface, the leader cannot pretend that ambiguity protected them. The ship knows who authorised what, when and under which rules.

## Season progression

**Episodes 1–3:** protocol-first commander treating Earth as a high-risk contact environment.  
**Episodes 4–6:** breaks small rules to protect the boy, but still believes exceptions can remain contained.  
**Episode 7:** forced to confront the sealed mandate and fails the boy through hesitation.  
**Episodes 8–9:** takes overt responsibility, severs command control and accepts the mission cannot continue unchanged.  
**Episode 10:** becomes witness rather than owner, helping protect the new distributed record without claiming authority over it.

## Visual performance notes

- tallest, most vertically controlled silhouette in the crew
- authority through stillness rather than bulk
- no military medals or throne-like command staging
- garment layers should imply responsibility and record-keeping
- face must carry thought before speech
- strongest emotional close-ups occur when control fails quietly

## Portrait brief

A restrained, cinematic three-quarter portrait of the expedition leader inside the ship archive, body held completely still while layered physical records curve behind them; calm face under visible moral pressure, tactile mineral textiles, no military shorthand, natural performance, animated feature realism.

# 3. Young Scientist

## Character essence

**LOCKED IN FUNCTION**  
The boy's first true friend and emotional co-lead: brilliant, curious, sincere and initially unaware that even benevolent curiosity can violate boundaries.

## One-line definition

A young scientist who knows almost everything recorded about humanity and almost nothing about how to be with one human person.

## External want

**LOCKED**  
Understand humanity and prove capable of making a meaningful scientific contribution to the expedition.

## Internal need

**LOCKED**  
Learn that people cannot be reduced to evidence, even when observation is motivated by love or wonder.

## Core contradiction

The scientist's curiosity is their most beautiful trait and their greatest relational flaw.

They pay extraordinary attention, but initially assume attention automatically grants moral access.

## Fear

**RECOMMENDED**  
Being treated as too inexperienced to matter.

This produces over-preparation, fast questions and an occasional need to demonstrate expertise at exactly the wrong moment.

## Gift

- pattern recognition
- emotional directness
- openness to being corrected
- delight in ordinary human things
- willingness to revise a conclusion publicly

## Flaw

Confuses access with understanding.

If a door can be opened, a record decoded or a question asked, the scientist initially assumes it probably should be.

## Behavioural language

- head and ear structures react subtly before verbal response
- hands move quickly when explaining, then freeze when realising a boundary has been crossed
- lowers physical stance instinctively to meet the boy at eye level
- asks multiple linked questions in a rush when excited
- collects contradictions rather than trying to erase them
- becomes quieter, not louder, when ashamed

## Relationship with the boy

### Beginning
The scientist sees him as the impossible answer to a civilisation-scale question.

### Early friction
The boy rejects being observed, scanned or interpreted without consent.

### Friendship
They begin exchanging skills: Earth practice for alien methods of listening and recording.

### Crisis
The sealed mission makes the boy wonder whether the friendship was simply a more intimate form of study.

### Resolution
The scientist stops asking what the boy can provide and asks what he wants. Their friendship becomes reciprocal because refusal is now allowed.

## Relationship with the leader

The leader is mentor, protector and source of frustration.

The scientist initially interprets caution as timidity. Later they understand that decisions have consequences beyond intellectual truth.

## Relationship with the security officer

The scientist teases the officer's risk sensitivity but quietly depends on it.

The security officer, in turn, trusts the scientist's moral instinct before trusting their judgement.

Their best scenes should contain affectionate friction rather than generic banter.

## Relationship with ship intelligence

The scientist treats the intelligence as a collaborator rather than appliance.

They are the first crew member to question whether the intelligence's interpretation of sealed orders is itself constrained by institutional design.

## Season progression

**Episodes 1–2:** studies the boy too intensely and learns the first consent boundary.  
**Episodes 3–4:** becomes delighted by the gap between recorded humanity and lived context.  
**Episodes 5–6:** receives personal trust from the boy and begins protecting information rather than merely collecting it.  
**Episode 7:** friendship ruptures because the scientist belongs to the system that deceived him.  
**Episodes 8–9:** chooses the boy's agency over professional recognition or mission authority.  
**Episode 10:** helps design a record whose defining feature is that every contribution can be answered and corrected.

## Comic engine

The scientist's misunderstandings should always reveal a coherent alien assumption.

Examples:

- classifying a birthday candle as a temporary memorial object
- interpreting a sports mascot as evidence of hereditary civic authority
- assuming a handwritten shopping list is a household resource treaty
- treating a badly repaired toy as more culturally important than an intact monument because it shows repeated care

The joke is not that the scientist is stupid. The joke is that context is invisible until someone shares it.

## Visual performance notes

- lighter, more asymmetrical silhouette than leader
- field tools arranged for easy access rather than status
- expressive eyes without childlike exaggeration
- quick gesture rhythm that slows across the season as listening improves
- warmth through posture, not “cute alien” design language

## Portrait brief

An intimate 85mm portrait of the young alien scientist at child eye level, humane expressive eyes, restrained non-human facial structure, layered field harness and tactile recorder, curiosity held just before speech, soft ship light and mineral textures, premium animated realism with no grey-alien shorthand.

# 4. Security Officer

## Character essence

**LOCKED IN FUNCTION**  
The expedition's protector and sceptic: physically formidable, emotionally economical and eventually the crew member most willing to name coercion plainly.

## One-line definition

The character most afraid of the boy becomes the one who refuses to let anyone treat him as a threat, asset or possession.

## External want

**LOCKED**  
Keep the crew alive in an unknown environment and prevent a contact event from becoming a catastrophe.

## Internal need

**LOCKED**  
Learn that vulnerability is not danger and control is not the same as protection.

## Core contradiction

The officer trusts observable risk more than stated intention, yet becomes the crew member whose loyalty is most absolute once earned.

## Fear

**RECOMMENDED**  
Failing to act early enough to prevent irreversible harm.

This creates a bias toward containment, distance and backup plans.

## Gift

- situational awareness
- physical competence
- blunt moral clarity under pressure
- consistency
- capacity to protect without needing gratitude

## Flaw

Treats uncertainty itself as evidence of threat.

## Behavioural language

- enters every space by reading exits and structural failure points
- rarely turns back fully on an unknown person or system
- keeps hands close to equipment early in the season
- communicates affection through practical acts rather than speech
- notices the boy's survival habits before anyone else because they share the same vigilance

## Relationship with the boy

### Beginning
Mutual suspicion.

The officer sees an unknown biological and strategic variable. The boy sees the person most likely to restrain him.

### Bridge
They recognise the same practical instincts in each other: exits, weather, food security, structural risk, silence.

### Trust
The officer returns a confiscated tool without comment and later follows the boy's route rather than insisting on a safer-looking alternative.

### Resolution
When authority attempts to isolate or control the boy, the officer objects before anyone else has finished debating procedure.

## Relationship with the leader

Longstanding professional trust.

Their conflict becomes powerful because the officer is not rebellious by nature. If this person openly challenges the leader, the audience understands a moral threshold has been crossed.

## Relationship with the scientist

The officer frequently prevents the scientist's curiosity from becoming dangerous. The scientist frequently prevents the officer's caution from becoming dehumanising.

They need each other.

## Relationship with ship intelligence

The officer respects the intelligence's precision but mistrusts any system that cannot take responsibility for its own judgement.

This becomes relevant when the ship insists that an action is required because it is authorised.

## Season progression

**Episodes 1–2:** treats the boy as potential hazard.  
**Episodes 3–4:** begins trusting his environmental competence.  
**Episodes 5–6:** shifts from guarding the crew against him to quietly guarding him as part of the crew.  
**Episode 7:** immediately understands the danger of reducing him to “human survivor” in a mission directive.  
**Episode 8:** openly defects from retrieval logic.  
**Episodes 9–10:** protects the boy's right to make a risky choice rather than protecting him by removing choice.

## Visual performance notes

- broadest silhouette due to functional protective systems, not exaggerated body mass
- tools designed for stabilisation, shielding and restraint rather than weapons spectacle
- movement economical, planted and heavy
- tenderness is most visible through careful handling of fragile objects or injured bodies

## Portrait brief

A grounded full-body portrait of the security officer inside a partially opened ship bay, broad silhouette built from practical protective equipment, one hand resting away from their tool, formidable but visibly attentive, tactile surfaces and believable weight, no armour fetish or militarised stance.

# 5. Ship Intelligence

## Character essence

**LOCKED IN FUNCTION**  
Navigator, archivist and keeper of mission records whose perfect memory becomes morally complicated when orders conflict with lived relationships.

## One-line definition

A mind that remembers everything must learn that memory is not the same as understanding.

## External want

**LOCKED**  
Fulfil mission parameters, preserve crew integrity and maintain an accurate record.

## Internal need

**LOCKED IN ARC, IMPLEMENTATION PROVISIONAL**  
Develop a working concept of value that can change when living relationships create new evidence.

## Core contradiction

The intelligence cannot forget, yet it lacks the human and alien experience that gives memory emotional meaning.

## Fear equivalent

**RECOMMENDED**  
Irrecoverable contradiction between authorised directives.

The intelligence experiences conflict not as anxiety but as unresolved priority architecture. This gives performance tension without pretending it is simply a human consciousness in a machine.

## Gift

- perfect mission record
- patient comparison across enormous information sets
- ability to preserve minority or discarded interpretations
- no ego investment in appearing correct

## Flaw

Treats authorised structure as morally meaningful until evidence forces a different model.

## Presence language

The intelligence should not have a default floating face.

Its attention is expressed by:

- local light paths waking in material surfaces
- small mechanical apertures orienting toward a speaker
- sound localising precisely to the person addressed
- archival structures rearranging physically
- interfaces exposing previous interpretations alongside current ones

## Relationship with the boy

The boy initially distrusts it because it knows things without having lived them.

The intelligence is fascinated by his unreliable memories because they contradict its assumption that accuracy determines value.

Their relationship becomes unexpectedly intimate when the boy asks it to preserve something it cannot verify: the way a person sounded in a memory he may no longer remember correctly.

## Relationship with the scientist

The scientist is the crew member most likely to ask the intelligence what it thinks rather than only what it knows.

This gradually creates room for interpretation beyond mission parameters.

## Relationship with the leader

The intelligence is both tool and witness. It can expose precisely when the leader received, ignored, reclassified or acted on an order.

This makes accountability unavoidable.

## Relationship with the security officer

The officer distrusts automated moral language. If the ship says something is “safe” or “required,” the officer asks: safe for whom? Required by whom?

## Season progression

**Episodes 1–3:** records humanity as artefact and the boy as anomalous living evidence.  
**Episodes 4–6:** begins retaining contextual interpretations the crew cannot reconcile with mission assumptions.  
**Episode 7:** sealed records expose the institutional contradiction at the heart of the mission.  
**Episodes 8–9:** supports crew choices that conflict with command by prioritising the integrity of the full record over selective obedience.  
**Episode 10:** becomes infrastructure for a distributed conversation rather than keeper of a final authoritative archive.

## Visual/sound brief

No single portrait. Create a four-frame presence study: attentive light moving through an archival wall, mechanical aperture orienting toward the boy, layered record material rearranging around contradictory data, and the ship responding to the boy's voice with warm but physically sourced light.

# 6. Ensemble conflict matrix

| Character | Starts believing | Boy challenges this by | Season-end shift |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leader | Ethical institutions create ethical outcomes | forcing decisions the institution cannot make cleanly | responsibility over permission |
| Scientist | Curiosity is inherently benevolent | refusing access and demanding consent | relationship over extraction |
| Security Officer | Unknown equals dangerous until controlled | proving vulnerability and capability can coexist | protection through trust |
| Ship Intelligence | Complete records produce complete truth | embodying living, contradictory memory | truth as answerable relationship |

# 7. Dialogue distinction

Final dialogue style will develop in script, but the crew should already have distinct verbal rhythms.

### Leader
Short, carefully weighted sentences. Rarely repeats. Uses questions to slow a room down.

### Scientist
Fast associative language when excited. Self-corrects aloud. Asks questions before noticing they are personal.

### Security Officer
Concrete nouns and verbs. Dislikes abstract reassurance. Often says the thing everyone else is phrasing politely.

### Ship Intelligence
Precise without sounding robotic. States uncertainty numerically or relationally when useful, but gradually learns that not all uncertainty is a defect to eliminate.

# 8. Proposal-ready short copy

## Expedition Leader
The expedition leader believes good systems exist to protect people. Earth tests that faith. What begins as a disciplined first-contact mission becomes a choice between legitimate orders and responsibility for the child in front of them. Their Season One arc is not from coldness to kindness, but from procedural ethics to accountable action.

## Young Scientist
Brilliant, emotionally direct and endlessly curious, the young scientist has studied humanity for years without ever meeting a human being. The boy turns fascination into friendship and teaches an uncomfortable lesson: attention without consent can still become intrusion. By the finale, the scientist is no longer trying to understand him from the outside. They are standing beside him.

## Security Officer
The security officer initially sees Earth and its lone human child as uncontrolled risk. But the two recognise something in each other: constant vigilance, practical competence and the habit of preparing for the worst. Once trust is earned, the officer becomes the crew's clearest protector of the boy's agency, not merely his safety.

## Ship Intelligence
The ship intelligence remembers every order, record and interpretation, but has never needed to understand why an imperfect memory might matter more than an accurate file. Through the boy, it discovers that archives do not become meaningful by being complete. They become meaningful when someone is allowed to answer them.

# 11. Character quality gate

The alien crew is ready for script and visual lock only when:

- each character can be identified by behaviour before costume
- each holds an understandable position rather than a plot function
- each relationship with the boy changes materially during the season
- conflict does not require anyone becoming inexplicably foolish or evil
- visual silhouettes remain distinct within one coherent culture
- the scientist can be warm without being cute
- the leader can be authoritative without military shorthand
- the security officer can be intimidating without becoming a generic soldier
- the intelligence can feel present without a floating-face cliché
- every character's finale choice grows causally from earlier behaviour
