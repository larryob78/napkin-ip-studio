# THE LAST BOY ON EARTH
## Tencent Development Decision Sheet v0.1

**Purpose:** Convert the remaining open canon questions into a controlled development sprint without prematurely locking unsupported story facts.

## 1. Decisions required before scripting

### A. The boy
Lock:
- name
- exact age within the current 11–13 range
- how long he has lived alone
- who raised him before the series begins
- the practical mechanism that kept him alive
- what he knows, suspects and falsely believes about humanity's disappearance

**Decision test:** His survival must feel emotionally credible, physically plausible within the series world and active rather than accidental.

### B. Humanity's disappearance
Choose one causal model that can support all currently established elements:
- the hidden human settlement
- the technology concealed on Earth
- the expedition's sealed retrieval orders
- the approaching pursuers
- the possibility that the boy was deliberately protected

**Decision test:** The explanation must deepen the story's question about what humanity is worth preserving. It must not reduce the series to a generic invasion plot.

### C. The Golden Record signal
Lock:
- what reactivated or amplified the signal
- who intended the expedition to find it
- why it became discoverable at this moment
- whether the signal is an invitation, warning, trap or incomplete message

**Decision test:** The signal must arise from a character choice or historic human decision, not coincidence.

### D. Alien expedition culture
Lock:
- names and visual silhouettes
- home culture and mission authority
- why this crew was selected
- the moral cost of disobeying orders
- the design grammar shared by anatomy, clothing, tools, ship and interfaces

**Decision test:** Every crew member must have a distinct emotional position on the boy and a credible reason to change.

## 2. Recommended reveal ladder

This is a structural recommendation, not locked canon.

1. **Episodes 1–2:** The boy recognises the Golden Record and conceals knowledge of repeating signals.
2. **Episodes 3–4:** Physical evidence suggests another intelligent visitor has moved through Earth recently.
3. **Episode 5:** A message addresses the boy personally, proving his isolation is part of a larger design.
4. **Episode 6:** His former home shows that he was hidden deliberately.
5. **Episode 7:** The expedition's retrieval order connects alien authority to the concealed human technology.
6. **Episode 8:** A human settlement beyond Earth is confirmed.
7. **Episode 9:** Using the technology exposes the boy and crew to an approaching force.
8. **Episode 10:** The crew rejects its original mission, sends a new record and receives a human response.

## 3. Two-minute proof decisions

The proof of concept does not need to solve the disappearance mystery. It must lock:
- the boy's face, age, clothing and performance style
- the young scientist's final design language
- the physical and sonic treatment of the Golden Record
- one reclaimed-Earth location family
- the balance of 70% intimate mystery and 30% epic scale
- the final quality threshold for eye-lines, skin, cloth, foliage, water and atmosphere

## 4. Tencent development sprint outputs

1. Locked one-page mythology statement
2. Named four-character expedition roster
3. Boy biography and survival logic
4. Season reveal map with cause-and-effect links
5. Final character and technology design grammar
6. Two-minute script and shot list
7. Production test budget and schedule
8. Rights, ownership and approval pathway

## 5. Quality gates

Do not approve a story decision unless it:
- changes a character relationship
- creates a later consequence
- preserves wonder before action
- avoids familiar invasion shorthand
- supports at least two episodes rather than one reveal
- can be communicated visually, not only through exposition

## Weakest remaining section
The single causal mythology linking the human settlement, hidden technology, sealed alien orders and incoming pursuers remains unresolved. This is now the highest-priority writing decision.
