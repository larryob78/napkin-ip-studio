# THE LAST BOY ON EARTH
## Reclaimed Earth World Bible v0.1

**Status:** Development manuscript  
**Purpose:** Define the reusable Earth environments, ecology, physical rules and visual storytelling system for the 50-page Series Development Proposal.  
**Canon rule:** This document develops the established reclaimed-Earth premise without locking the date of humanity's disappearance, the final causal mythology, the boy's identity, or whether other humans remain on Earth.

---

# 1. The governing idea

Earth is not a dead world. It is a living world from which organised human civilisation has vanished.

The emotional power of the setting comes from the collision of two truths:

1. Humanity is absent.
2. Human life is still visible everywhere.

A school timetable remains beneath moss. A kitchen table is set beneath a collapsed roof. A tram line has become a wetland. Trees grow through offices whose screens are long dark. These places must never feel like generic apocalypse scenery. They are evidence of interrupted lives.

Nature is not the antagonist and not a decorative layer. It has reorganised the planet. Water, roots, weather, animals and seasonal growth now determine movement through spaces once designed for people.

The world should create wonder before danger.

---

# 2. Locked world principles

## 2.1 Recognisable, transformed, specific

Locations must retain enough real human identity to be emotionally legible while becoming unfamiliar through time and ecological change. Dublin is not a nameless ruined city. Its river, bridges, Georgian geometry, civic buildings, coastal weather and layered streets remain visible.

## 2.2 Reclamation, not destruction

The primary visual language is not explosions, ash or endless rubble. It is adaptation:

- roots widening old seams
- rain entering broken roofs
- birds occupying towers
- reeds filling transit routes
- salt and wind reshaping exposed structures
- human materials ageing at different rates
- plants following light through architecture

## 2.3 Every location carries a former human purpose

The boy knows spaces through use and risk. The aliens initially read them as archaeology. The audience should feel both perspectives.

Every major location therefore needs:

- its original human function
- its new ecological function
- its practical value to the boy
- the alien crew's initial misunderstanding
- one emotional trace of the people who used it
- one story hazard
- one mystery opportunity

## 2.4 Quiet is active

Silence is not emptiness. The world is filled with wind, insects, water, animal calls, settling structures and distant mechanical remnants. Human-made sound is rare and therefore powerful.

## 2.5 Scale serves intimacy

Wide images show the magnitude of the lost civilisation. Close images show what its loss means. An aerial of reclaimed Dublin should be followed by a hand touching a worn name label, a child's drawing, or condensation forming on an old photograph.

---

# 3. Environmental history without premature mythology

The world must show that the disappearance was not a single simple catastrophe, but this document does not lock its cause.

The environment can safely establish:

- civilisation-scale systems ceased operating long ago
- some local systems continued automatically for different lengths of time
- certain shelters, archives and signal sites were maintained more recently
- some areas show deliberate concealment rather than ordinary decay
- at least one non-human visitor moved through the city before the expedition
- weather and ecological succession have had enough time to transform urban space dramatically

Avoid environmental clues that force one unapproved answer. No universal burn layer, identical mass graves, single invasion symbol, or one visually obvious extinction event should appear unless later approved in canon.

---

# 4. Reclaimed Dublin: master environment family

Dublin provides the series with a world that is internationally legible but visually distinct from familiar post-apocalyptic cities.

## Core visual identity

- soft Atlantic light rather than permanent orange wasteland
- wet stone, brick, oxidised metal and reflective water
- Georgian facades fractured by roots and climbing plants
- the Liffey as a living corridor through the city
- coastal weather changing quickly within scenes
- old civic geometry disrupted by organic growth
- intimate streets opening unexpectedly into epic vistas

## Seasonal baseline

The preferred Season One baseline is late spring moving into early summer. This gives:

- abundant green without tropical excess
- cold mornings and warm late light
- rain, mist and clear intervals within one day
- nesting birds and active animal life
- flowers and new growth against old stone
- long evenings suitable for emotional golden-hour sequences

This is provisional and does not lock the exact calendar.

## Colour system

- deep wet greens
- slate blue sky and water
- pale limestone and grey concrete
- warm aged brick
- rust and oxidised copper
- amber sunlight used sparingly as emotional punctuation

Avoid constant teal-orange grading, radioactive greens and monochrome grey ruin imagery.

# 5. Location Family A: The Vertical City

## Definition

The city centre seen as a layered forest of towers, rooftops, bridges, elevated routes and hanging vegetation.

## Story function

This is the epic face of Earth and the boy's movement network. It establishes his competence, gives the aliens a terrain disadvantage and creates vertical danger without relying on combat.

## Reusable locations

### A1. Rooftop routes

The boy uses linked roofs, maintenance ladders, cables and improvised crossings to move above flooded or unstable streets.

**Former purpose:** maintenance access, offices, apartments and commercial roofs.  
**New function:** animal corridors, water collection, lookout points and safe travel.  
**Boy's value:** speed, warning distance and control of approach routes.  
**Alien misunderstanding:** the crew initially treats the routes as accidental rather than a designed survival network.  
**Emotional trace:** rooftop gardens, washing lines and abandoned personal spaces reveal ordinary domestic life.  
**Hazard:** wind, weakened surfaces and sudden visibility from above.  
**Mystery use:** signal marks, recently disturbed nests or unfamiliar landing traces.

### A2. The tower forest

Office and residential towers have become artificial cliffs. Trees grow from terraces. Birds and climbing animals occupy facades. Interior floors create stacked microclimates.

**Visual rule:** never cover every building evenly in vines. Different orientations, materials and water access produce different patterns of growth.

### A3. The Spire and civic axis

A surviving vertical landmark provides orientation across the city. Weather, vegetation and material ageing change its surface, but its silhouette remains unmistakable.

**Story value:** navigation, scale, transmission alignment and a possible high-risk signal location.

## Signature image

The boy and expedition stand on a roof above Dublin. The city stretches below as an inhabited ecosystem, not a graveyard. Their small silhouettes make the scale enormous, but their body language carries the emotional scene.

# 6. Location Family B: The River City

## Definition

The Liffey, canals, flooded tunnels and drainage routes form a connected water system through the ruins.

## Story function

Water creates beauty, access, danger and evidence. It preserves some objects, destroys others and moves clues between locations.

## Reusable locations

### B1. The living Liffey

The river is cleaner in some ways and wilder in others. Vegetation occupies embankments and disused quays. Tidal movement changes access routes twice daily.

**Boy's knowledge:** he reads tide lines, floating debris, bird behaviour and water colour.  
**Alien weakness:** orbital and technical mapping cannot replace local timing.  
**Emotional trace:** signs, benches, bicycles and fragments of public life remain along the quays.  
**Hazard:** cold water, hidden currents, unstable walls and rapid weather.  
**Mystery use:** an object arrives upstream that should not be moving.

### B2. The flooded transit concourse

Used in Episode 1 as the first full reveal of the boy.

**Visual identity:** shallow reflective water, broken signage, roots descending from ceiling cracks, narrow shafts of daylight and distant movement below the surface.

**Production rule:** water must react physically to footsteps, falling material, cloth and displaced air. It cannot behave like a static mirror.

### B3. Canal and service routes

Narrower waterways provide stealth, comedy and intimate travel. Old locks and gates create controllable barriers.

## Signature image

The Ha'penny Bridge appears through mist and vegetation, recognisable but transformed. The boy crosses below it by a route only he understands while the alien crew attempts a technologically elegant but impractical alternative.

# 7. Location Family C: Memory Interiors

## Definition

Protected or partially protected human interiors where ordinary life remains emotionally present.

## Story function

These spaces prevent the series from becoming a sequence of grand ruins. They reveal individuals, relationships and private life.

## Reusable locations

### C1. The museum ecosystem

Used in Episode 3.

**Visual identity:** a public interior transformed into a layered habitat while displays remain partially legible. The comedy of alien interpretation must sit beside genuine grief.

### C2. Domestic rooms

Bedrooms, kitchens, corridors and family storage spaces survive unevenly. The most powerful details are modest:

- height marks on a wall
- a repaired toy
- a handwritten shopping list
- shoes by a door
- a table worn by repeated use
- a photograph protected inside a drawer

These objects must not become sentimental decoration. Each one should alter a character's understanding or decision.

### C3. The voice archive

A place where damaged recordings activate unpredictably. Human memory is experienced as overlapping presence rather than clean exposition.

**Alien-cultural resonance:** the crew's memory-based culture makes this environment emotionally destabilising and deeply attractive.

### C4. The boy's neutral workroom

Introduced in Episode 2. This is not his true home.

**Function:** weather observation, mapping, repair and controlled communication.  
**Design:** disciplined, practical, accumulated over years. Changes in handwriting height and tool sophistication quietly show his growth.  
**Guardrail:** do not clutter it with generic survival props. Every item needs a function or relationship.

## Signature image

The boy and young scientist sit apart in a dark recording room while fragments of human voices move through the space. Their distance shows caution; their shared stillness shows connection.

# 8. Location Family D: Hidden Systems

## Definition

Archives, transmission sites, shelters and technology spaces that were deliberately maintained, concealed or accessed after civilisation disappeared.

## Story function

This family carries the season mystery from environmental archaeology into active conspiracy and choice.

## Reusable locations

### D1. Signal nodes

Human infrastructure producing weak, irregular or responsive transmissions.

**Visual rule:** signal technology should look like adapted human systems, not suddenly pristine science-fiction machinery.

### D2. Underground archive

Episode 5 destination. A protected space containing records that appear more recent than the surrounding city.

**Emotional function:** hope becomes personal when a message addresses the boy directly.

### D3. The former home or concealment site

Episode 6 destination. It must carry evidence of care, fear and deliberate preparation without yet determining who made every decision.

### D4. Broadcast facility

Episode 8 destination. Its scale and danger come from neglected infrastructure, exposed heights, stored energy and the possibility of transmitting beyond Earth.

## Guardrail

Hidden systems must not feel like a separate glossy futuristic layer. They are human-built, repaired, repurposed and compromised by time.

# 9. Ecology and animal life

Animals make the world feel continuous rather than abandoned.

## Principles

- use species and behaviours credible to the region unless a later timeline justifies change
- show adaptation through behaviour and habitat before extreme mutation
- avoid random oversized predators as a substitute for suspense
- animals often detect structural, weather or human-made changes before characters do
- the boy reads animal behaviour as practical information
- the aliens initially over-rely on scans and under-read behaviour

## Recurring ecological storytellers

### Birds

They provide scale, movement, warning and sudden silence. Nesting patterns reveal safe and unsafe structures.

### Foxes and urban mammals

They move confidently through former human routes, creating both companionship and competition for resources.

### Insects and pollinators

Close detail reinforces that life is abundant even when humanity is absent.

### Aquatic life

Waterways visibly support new systems. Their behaviour can reveal contamination, machinery or upstream disturbance.

---

# 10. Weather as dramatic structure

Dublin weather should change scenes rather than merely decorate them.

## Uses

- rain reveals leaks, tracks, recent movement and hidden writing
- mist reduces technological advantage and creates intimacy
- wind changes rooftop routes and sound direction
- sudden sunlight reveals scale or emotionally releases a scene
- cold affects the boy and aliens differently
- tide and storm timing create real deadlines

## Guardrail

Avoid permanent rain as shorthand for seriousness. The emotional range requires brightness, clear air, warm evening light and moments of startling beauty.

---

# 11. Human objects as story evidence

Every object belongs to one of four dramatic categories:

1. **Tool:** still useful in the present.
2. **Trace:** reveals ordinary past life.
3. **Memory:** emotionally connected to a character.
4. **Clue:** advances the disappearance mystery.

An object should not perform all four functions at once. Clear function keeps scenes readable.

## Alien interpretation rule

Misunderstanding must be logical. The crew observes material, wear and shape but lacks cultural context. Their errors should reveal their own values as much as human absence.

## Boy interpretation rule

The boy does not automatically understand all human culture. Depending on what he was taught and what he has experienced, some objects are as mysterious to him as to the aliens. This preserves discovery and prevents him becoming an exposition device.

---

# 12. Movement and survival logic

The boy's competence is environmental, learned and costly.

He survives through:

- route planning
- weather reading
- water collection
- maintenance
- observation
- hidden caches
- controlled risk
- retreat plans
- knowledge recorded over time

This document does not lock who taught him or how long he has been alone.

## Alien movement contrast

The crew possesses superior tools but initially lacks:

- local timing
- intuitive reading of decay
- understanding of human scale
- familiarity with unstable materials
- knowledge of animal warning behaviour
- emotional awareness of private spaces

Their growth is visible when they begin asking the boy where and how to move rather than treating him as cargo.

# 13. Sound world

## Natural layer

- wind in damaged structures
- water through pipes and tunnels
- wing movement
- insects
- distant animals
- rain on different human materials
- trees moving against architecture

## Human-remnant layer

Rare, fragile and emotionally charged:

- a mechanism completing an old cycle
- a damaged announcement
- music from a protected device
- recorded voices
- bells moved by weather
- a signal pulse

## Alien layer

The crew's sounds should feel tactile, restrained and culturally coherent with their layered memory technology. Avoid generic electronic beeps.

## Key principle

When human music enters, the soundscape should make room for it. The audience must feel that an organised human sound has returned to a world that has not heard it for a very long time.

---

# 14. Visual continuity checklist

Before approving any Earth image or sequence, confirm:

- the place is recognisably Dublin or intentionally identified otherwise
- vegetation follows light, water and structure rather than covering everything evenly
- architecture shows different rates and types of decay
- weather and surfaces interact physically
- at least one former human purpose remains legible
- the image contains story information, not only atmosphere
- the boy's route or practical relationship to the space is clear
- alien technology does not visually dominate Earth
- there is no generic apocalypse shorthand
- scale supports an emotional beat
- props and markings remain consistent with previous appearances

# 17. Open decisions preserved

The following remain deliberately unresolved:

- exact year and duration since organised civilisation disappeared
- final cause of the disappearance
- whether ecological conditions are entirely natural
- exact location and design of the boy's true home
- identity of whoever maintained the hidden systems
- whether other humans remain on Earth
- nature of the earlier non-human visitor

These decisions require the causal mythology paper and later canon approval.

---

# World quality test

The world is working only when a location does all three:

1. creates visual wonder;
2. changes a relationship or decision;
3. reveals something about humanity without explaining it in dialogue.
