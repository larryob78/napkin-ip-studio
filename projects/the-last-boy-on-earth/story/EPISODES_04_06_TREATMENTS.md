# THE LAST BOY ON EARTH
## Episodes 4–6 Full Dramatic Treatments

**Status:** Development draft v0.1  
**Format:** 10 episodes × approximately 15 minutes  
**Canon rule:** This document expands approved season beats without locking the boy’s name, exact age, the final disappearance cause, the identity of any surviving humans, or the ultimate purpose of the hidden technology.

---

# EPISODE 4 — THE CITY THAT GREW BACK

## Episode purpose

The relationship story moves from cautious cooperation to earned loyalty. The reclaimed city stops being scenery and becomes an active force that tests the crew’s assumptions about Earth, the boy and command authority.

## Human truth

The world continues after us, but care still leaves a mark.

## Opening image

At dawn, the expedition looks across a city whose rooftops have become a forest canopy. Towers rise like cliffs through mist. Far below, flooded streets reflect birds, vines and fragments of old signs.

## Sequence 1 — The route only he can see

The crew must cross the city to reach a signal relay identified on the boy’s hand-drawn map. Their scans identify the shortest route: a broad elevated transport line. The boy refuses it and chooses a slower path through rooftops, service bridges and collapsed interiors.

The security officer assumes he is being difficult. The young scientist notices that the boy is reading small environmental signs the ship cannot interpret: nesting behaviour, water stains, shifting plant growth and the sound of structural movement. His knowledge is embodied, not theoretical.

The leader allows the boy’s route, partly to build trust and partly because the city has already defeated their remote models.

**Character movement:** The boy becomes an active guide rather than a passenger.  
**Visual opportunity:** Tiny figures crossing a green canyon between buildings.

## Sequence 2 — Human systems become ecosystems

They enter a former commercial district where roots have split concrete, birds nest inside screens and rainwater runs through buildings as new rivers. The aliens repeatedly misread human infrastructure as deliberate ecological design.

A comic beat becomes emotional when the young scientist praises humanity for building spaces that now protect thousands of living things. The boy quietly explains that people did not plan any of this. Nature simply used what remained.

The exchange deepens the scientist’s admiration while complicating the crew’s idealised picture of humanity.

**Human discovery:** Meaning can outlive intention.  
**Mystery progression:** Several areas show signs of recent pruning or obstruction removal, suggesting intelligent maintenance.

## Sequence 3 — The living bridge

The route narrows to a suspended pedestrian link wrapped in mature roots. The boy crosses first. A hidden animal colony erupts from the structure, triggering a chain reaction. The bridge tears loose at one end.

The boy falls onto a lower level and becomes trapped as water rises through a broken service chamber. Protocol requires the expedition leader to preserve the crew and mission equipment. The security officer advises withdrawal and remote retrieval.

The leader breaks protocol and goes after the boy personally.

The rescue is physical, quiet and costly. The leader abandons a piece of mission equipment to hold open a failing structure. The boy sees the choice.

**Character turn:** Authority becomes personal responsibility.  
**Later consequence:** The abandoned equipment can be recovered or detected by another force.

## Sequence 4 — What the city remembers

Sheltering from heavy rain, the group enters a high office floor transformed into a wetland garden. The boy reveals that he once crossed this area with someone else, but stops before saying who.

The young scientist does not push him. Instead, they share a found object from the old world: a cheap plastic desk toy still capable of a tiny repetitive motion. The object has no strategic value, but the boy keeps it.

The leader observes the exchange and begins to understand that collecting human artefacts is not enough. The crew must learn why certain things matter.

## Sequence 5 — The repeating voice

At sunset, they reach the relay structure. It appears dead. The boy climbs into a maintenance cavity and manually restores a mechanical connection. The system wakes with a pulse that spreads across the city.

A repeating transmission emerges in a human language. It is damaged, incomplete and recent enough to challenge the assumption that Earth is silent.

The boy freezes before the others do.

The young scientist notices.

## Ending image

The crew stands on the relay platform as the same human phrase repeats across the darkening city. Far away, lights answer in a pattern that could be electrical failure, automated infrastructure or something deliberate.

## Episode consequences

- The leader has broken protocol for the boy.
- The boy’s local knowledge is now essential to the mission.
- Evidence of maintenance complicates the empty-Earth assumption.
- The transmission moves the story from archaeological wonder into active mystery.
- The young scientist suspects the boy knows more than he admits.

## Canon protection

This treatment does not define who maintains the city, who sent the message or whether another human is physically present.

# EPISODE 5 — A VOICE IN THE DARK

## Episode purpose

This is the season midpoint. Hope becomes specific, dangerous and emotionally destabilising. The boy’s secrecy is revealed as fear rather than manipulation.

## Human truth

Hope can hurt more than certainty because it gives loss somewhere to return.

## Opening image

In darkness, the transmission plays inside the alien ship. Each repetition is cleaned, separated and analysed. The boy sits beyond the light, listening without looking at anyone.

## Sequence 1 — The message beneath the message

The crew isolates coordinates hidden in the repeating signal. They point to an underground archive beneath a former civic district. The security officer argues that the signal may be bait. The leader insists on investigating because it may be the first living trace of humanity.

The boy refuses to go.

When asked why, he claims the tunnels are unsafe. His body language makes clear that this is not the whole truth.

The young scientist chooses to remain with him while the others prepare. Rather than interrogate him, the scientist plays a fragment from the Golden Record. The shared music creates space for honesty, but the boy still cannot speak.

## Sequence 2 — Descent

The group enters the underground through a flooded transit station. Human wayfinding symbols have become abstract marks to the aliens, while the boy reads them instantly. He eventually joins because he knows the route and cannot bear to let them go alone.

The descent moves through three layers:

1. public ruins overtaken by water and roots
2. sealed civic infrastructure that remains strangely intact
3. a hidden archive designed to survive long after the surrounding city failed

The deeper they go, the less reclaimed the environment becomes. This is not simply a ruin. It has been protected.

**Visual opportunity:** warm alien light moving through black water and preserved human signage.

## Sequence 3 — The archive of ordinary life

Inside, the crew expects military or scientific records. Instead, the archive contains personal material: school recordings, domestic video, voice messages, local radio, recipes, jokes and unfinished conversations.

The aliens are overwhelmed by the scale of uncurated human life. Their own culture preserves memory through formal public selection. Humanity has left behind a chaotic ocean of private moments.

The young scientist calls it beautiful. The boy finds it unbearable.

He disconnects a playback system after a family voice resembles someone he remembers.

**Character movement:** The scientist stops romanticising preservation and sees its emotional cost.

## Sequence 4 — Addressed to him

The signal source activates when the boy enters a restricted chamber. A human voice begins a message. Damage obscures most of it, but one element is undeniable: it is addressed directly to the boy.

The crew turns toward him.

He admits he has heard versions of the signal before. He has followed fragments of it across the city for a long time, but every trail ended in silence. He stopped believing because hope made survival harder.

He does not know who is speaking or whether the message is live, automated or old.

**Midpoint revelation:** His isolation may be part of a larger design, and he may not be the last human.

## Sequence 5 — The archive chooses

The system attempts to transmit a response. The security officer blocks it, warning that any active reply could expose their position. The leader agrees to delay transmission until they understand the risk.

The boy experiences this as another adult authority deciding whether he is allowed to seek his own people.

The young scientist sides with him emotionally but cannot dismiss the danger. Their first serious disagreement opens between them.

The archive then reveals a partial route to a place connected to the boy’s past.

## Ending image

The boy watches a frozen frame from the damaged message. Most of the speaker’s face is lost to interference. One hand remains visible, making a gesture the boy recognises.

He whispers that he knows where they have to go.

## Episode consequences

- The crew learns the boy concealed prior knowledge of the signal.
- The boy’s secrecy is reframed as trauma and self-protection.
- A message addresses him personally.
- The archive demonstrates deliberate long-term preservation.
- The possibility of human survivors becomes credible but unconfirmed.
- The crew is divided over whether to answer.

## Canon protection

The speaker’s identity, message age, transmission origin and relationship to the boy remain unresolved.

# EPISODE 6 — HOME

## Episode purpose

The mystery becomes intimate. The crew discovers that the boy was not merely lucky to survive. Someone made choices to hide, teach and protect him. His grief becomes visible, and the alien mission’s moral compromise begins to surface.

## Human truth

Home is not where nothing changes. It is where change still has the power to hurt.

## Opening image

The boy leads the crew beyond the city centre into a quieter district where houses, schools and gardens have merged into woodland. He walks ahead, moving faster as recognition replaces caution.

## Sequence 1 — The boundary

They reach an area concealed by ordinary-looking environmental barriers: collapsed walls, planted thorn growth, redirected water and false dead ends. To the aliens, the pattern is invisible until the boy demonstrates it.

The route was built to look accidental.

The leader recognises strategic concealment. The security officer recognises defensive planning. The young scientist recognises care.

The boy says only that this is where he used to live.

## Sequence 2 — A home built for continuation

The home is not a bunker in the conventional sense. It is a network linking a domestic living space, a hidden garden, water collection, mechanical storage and a small learning room. Human warmth and survival engineering coexist.

Objects establish absent relationships without defining them completely:

- height marks on a wall
- repaired clothing in several sizes
- lessons recorded in different voices
- a table set for more than one person
- emergency instructions rewritten by hand
- a rule that every stored item must have two uses

The boy moves through the rooms performing old habits automatically.

## Sequence 3 — The scientist sees the child

The young scientist finds a small sleeping area preserved exactly as it was left. Until now, the scientist has viewed the boy as survivor, guide and mystery. Here, they finally see the scale of his childhood.

The boy tries to turn the room into a tour. He explains systems, supplies and escape routes. The scientist asks no factual question. They sit beside him.

He admits that he did not stay here because the silence became too large. The city, dangerous as it was, felt less empty.

This is the first time he allows another character to witness grief without converting it into useful information.

**Character turn:** Their bond becomes reciprocal rather than observational.

## Sequence 4 — He was hidden

The crew discovers that the home’s systems were designed to conceal one specific inhabitant. Sensors, routes and stored materials were calibrated around the boy’s movement and growth. The protection was deliberate and long-term.

A sealed compartment contains fragments of a larger plan, but crucial identifiers are removed or corrupted. It proves intention without explaining motive.

The security officer raises the most frightening possibility: the boy may have been hidden not only from danger, but from someone searching for him.

## Sequence 5 — The mission knew

While examining the home’s concealed technology, the ship intelligence detects a pattern that matches encrypted data inside the expedition’s own mission records.

The leader accesses the records and finds evidence that Earth was never an entirely unknown destination. Their authority possessed information connected to the hidden site and withheld it from the crew.

The leader closes the file before everyone can see the full contents.

The boy notices the hesitation.

The trust gained in Episode 4 begins to fracture.

## Sequence 6 — Leaving home again

A power system reactivates and sends a brief pulse. The home may now be detectable. The crew must leave quickly.

The boy chooses one object to carry. It should be ordinary, emotionally legible and visually reusable across the series. The exact object remains a design decision.

Before departing, he changes one thing in the room rather than preserving it untouched. This small act shows that he is beginning to live forward rather than only guard the past.

## Ending image

From outside, the hidden home disappears again into woodland. Inside the alien ship, the leader privately opens the sealed mission file. A retrieval instruction appears beside a symbol that also exists within the boy’s home.

## Episode consequences

- The boy’s survival was deliberately engineered.
- His home demonstrates a network of care without identifying its creators.
- The young scientist becomes his first true confidant.
- The security officer begins treating the boy as a protected person rather than a mission variable.
- Alien command is linked to knowledge hidden on Earth.
- The leader now carries a secret that can destroy the group’s trust.

## Canon protection

This treatment does not identify who raised the boy, why he was selected, what the hidden technology does or why alien authority knows about it.

# CROSS-EPISODE DRAMATIC ENGINE

## Relationship progression

- **Episode 4:** The leader chooses the boy over protocol.
- **Episode 5:** The boy reveals that hope itself has become frightening.
- **Episode 6:** The scientist witnesses his grief, while the leader begins withholding truth.

The result is deliberate emotional counterpoint: intimacy grows at the same moment institutional trust begins to collapse.

## Mystery progression

1. The city shows signs of recent maintenance.
2. A human transmission proves Earth is not entirely silent.
3. The message addresses the boy personally.
4. His former home proves deliberate concealment.
5. Alien mission records contain prior knowledge of the hidden site.

Each reveal is caused by a character choice and produces a later consequence.

## Visual progression

- Episode 4 is vertical, open and weather-driven.
- Episode 5 descends into darkness, water and preserved memory.
- Episode 6 becomes intimate, domestic and emotionally specific.

This creates a useful visual rhythm for the Greenlight Book: epic world, buried mystery, human-scale grief.

## Production-facing hero images

1. The crew crossing a root-bound bridge high above reclaimed Dublin.
2. The leader reaching the boy inside a collapsing flooded structure.
3. The team descending through a black-water transit station.
4. The boy surrounded by thousands of preserved human voices.
5. A damaged human face addressing him through archive interference.
6. The hidden woodland home revealed through layered camouflage.
7. The boy and young scientist sitting in the preserved sleeping room.
8. The sealed alien mission file matching a symbol from the home.

## Remaining decision dependencies

Before final scripts, the following must be approved:

- the boy’s identity and exact survival timeline
- the form and emotional identity of the message speaker
- the ordinary object he takes from home
- the strongest causal mythology linking concealment, off-world humans, hidden technology, alien authority and the approaching force
