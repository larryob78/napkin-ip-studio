# THE LAST BOY ON EARTH
## Story Trust Episode Pass v1

**Project:** The Last Boy on Earth  
**Format:** 10 × approximately 15 minutes  
**Status:** In Review  
**Canon read:** CANON.md, SEASON_ONE_MAP.md, CORE_CHARACTERS.md, LBOE-DEVELOPMENT-LOCKS-v0.2.md  

## Story Trust mandate

This pass strengthens emotional distinction, causal progression, episode endings and character continuity without promoting provisional mythology into locked canon.

### Season spine
Eli begins the season believing that being left behind means he was not worth saving. The Vael arrive believing humanity can be understood through its greatest cultural artefact. Across ten episodes, both assumptions are dismantled. Eli learns that survival without connection is another kind of disappearance; the crew learns that preserving a civilisation means accepting responsibility for a living person, not merely collecting its memory.

### Escalation rule
Every episode must deliver:
1. one unforgettable physical discovery;
2. one emotionally costly choice;
3. one changed relationship;
4. one reveal caused by character action;
5. one final image or question that compels the next episode.

# EPISODE 1 — THE SIGNAL

## Dramatic question
Will Eli run from the first living beings he has seen in eighteen months, or risk being found?

## Synopsis
Tavi Renn has spent years studying the Voyager Golden Record, drawn less to its science than to the vulnerability of a species introducing itself to strangers. When Tavi identifies the Record’s mathematical language hidden inside an anomalous signal from Earth, Commander Sela Venn authorises a limited expedition. The Vael expect archaeological contact at worst and living humanity at best. Instead, they descend over a breathtaking Dublin transformed into forest, water and broken stone. There are no broadcasts, no vehicles, no crowds—only an active signal pulsing beneath the city.

Eli Byrne watches the landing from concealment. He has survived by treating every unexplained sound as danger, and the arrival confirms his oldest fear: something has finally found him. When Tavi becomes separated during a structural collapse, Eli could leave the alien to die. Instead, he intervenes, revealing himself for a few seconds before fleeing. Tavi follows not with a weapon but with a fragment of music from the Golden Record. Eli stops. The melody is one his caretakers used to play inside the refuge.

## Character movement
- Eli chooses curiosity over total isolation for the first time.
- Tavi stops thinking of humanity as an abstract civilisation and sees one frightened child.
- Sela’s archaeological mission becomes a living-contact crisis.

## Closing hook
Eli softly completes the melody Tavi is playing—then asks, in a voice barely used for eighteen months, “Why did you come back?”

## Hero image
Eli and Tavi facing one another across a rain-filled, overgrown station concourse while the Golden Record’s music echoes through the ruin.

# EPISODE 2 — THE VISITORS

## Dramatic question
Can trust begin before language does?

## Synopsis
The crew brings Eli aboard only because the storm and failing city structures make the surface unsafe. He experiences the Vael ship as both miracle and trap. ARO attempts translation through archived human languages, but Eli’s isolation, damaged speech rhythms and fear produce dangerous misunderstandings. Orro Kest sees an unknown biological risk. Sela insists on procedure. Tavi keeps asking questions faster than Eli can answer.

The breakthrough comes through food. Eli rejects the ship’s fabricated meal, then shows the crew how to prepare something simple from supplies in his pack. The Vael misunderstand the ritual, but Tavi recognises that Eli is not merely feeding them—he is testing whether they will share vulnerability. When Orro quietly eats first, Eli relaxes. Later, he reveals a hand-drawn map of repeating signals across Dublin, each marked with dates. He has tracked them for years but never followed the strongest one.

## Character movement
- Eli permits the crew to see evidence of his private life.
- Orro makes the first protective gesture, despite remaining suspicious.
- Tavi learns that attention without restraint can feel like threat.

## Closing hook
ARO overlays Eli’s map with the expedition’s hidden signal data. Every pulse converges beneath a place Eli has deliberately scratched out.

## Hero image
A shared meal in the warm centre of the Vael ship, with human utensils, alien hands and Eli’s battered map spread between them.

# EPISODE 3 — MUSEUM OF EVERYTHING

## Dramatic question
What survives when nobody remains to explain why ordinary things mattered?

## Synopsis
To reach the first signal relay, Eli guides the crew through a ruined museum where he once scavenged batteries and tools. The Vael treat each object as cultural evidence, confidently misreading toys, sports equipment, domestic appliances and ceremonial displays. The humour gives Eli his first unguarded laugh, but the museum also contains personal memories he has avoided: a children’s exhibition visited with his caretaker family before the refuge failed.

When part of the building collapses, Orro has time to save either a mission scanner or a small box Eli has risked himself to retrieve. Orro chooses the box. Inside is a damaged family recording, proving that emotional value can outweigh strategic value. Elsewhere, Tavi finds fresh scoring on a sealed display and a repaired power conduit less than a year old. Someone intelligent has moved through the museum since Eli last visited.

## Character movement
- Eli laughs with another person for the first time since becoming alone.
- Orro begins valuing what Eli values.
- Tavi’s fascination becomes responsibility rather than collection.

## Closing hook
The restored family recording contains a background voice saying Eli’s name—followed by a date only six weeks old.

## Hero image
Orro holding a tiny damaged memory box in one enormous hand as Eli realises it has been saved.

# EPISODE 4 — THE CITY THAT GREW BACK

## Dramatic question
Can Eli accept help without believing it will make him weak?

## Synopsis
The new clue leads across central Dublin, where roads have become waterways and buildings form a living ecosystem. Eli insists on travelling by his established routes, refusing Vael assistance. His confidence is real, but so is the danger: the city has changed since his last crossing. A flooded transport tunnel collapses, separating Eli and Sela from the others.

Sela abandons mission protocol and removes a piece of Vael protective equipment to keep Eli alive, risking contamination and disciplinary consequences. Trapped together, Eli admits he has heard the repeating signal since before he became alone. He never followed it because his final caretaker warned him that answering could endanger “everyone who got away.” Sela promises not to force him forward—then gives him the choice. Eli chooses to continue.

## Character movement
- Eli learns that accepting help can be an active decision.
- Sela places Eli’s safety above mission purity.
- Their relationship shifts from specimen and commander toward child and guardian.

## Closing hook
At the relay, a human voice begins repeating through dead city speakers: “Eli Byrne. Living authentication required.”

## Hero image
Sela and Eli waist-deep in black floodwater beneath a collapsed tunnel, lit by the warm pulse of Vael emergency material.

# EPISODE 5 — A VOICE IN THE DARK

## Dramatic question
Is hope still hope when it asks for something dangerous?

## Synopsis
The crew descends into an underground archive built around an inactive section of the Chorus. The system recognises Eli’s presence but refuses access without a living neural response. Eli is furious that adults who concealed the truth are still making decisions for him through machines. Tavi argues that authentication may reveal survivors. Orro warns that the signal could be a trap. Sela gives Eli final authority.

Eli chooses a limited response. The archive wakes in fragments: domestic messages, evacuation instructions, maps and incomplete testimony from his caretakers. He learns that he was deliberately protected, not accidentally forgotten—but the message stops before explaining why. The emotional relief is immediately complicated by the archive detecting a faint human-origin transmission beyond Earth.

## Character movement
- Eli’s belief that he was worthless begins to crack.
- Tavi understands that discovering truth does not give them ownership of it.
- Sela formally recognises Eli as a decision-maker.

## Closing hook
The archive displays a single phrase: **HAVEN ACTIVE — LOCATION WITHHELD**. Then ARO privately detects a second signal responding to the authentication pulse.

## Hero image
Eli alone in archive darkness as thousands of human voices illuminate around him, while Tavi watches from a respectful distance.

# EPISODE 6 — HOME

## Dramatic question
Can Eli return to the place that made him without retreating into the child he was there?

## Synopsis
The archive points toward Eli’s former refuge beneath a residential district. Returning home destabilises him more than any physical threat. The space is painfully ordinary: height marks, repaired furniture, seed trays, lesson recordings and messages never meant for outsiders. Through these fragments, the crew sees that Eli was raised with care and trained deliberately to survive after the adults were gone.

Eli finds his caretaker’s final log. It explains that his neural signature could reopen the emergency Chorus pathway and that keeping him hidden protected the off-world survivors from being traced. The log never says when they intended to return. Eli interprets this as another abandonment. Tavi refuses to soften the evidence, but remains with him through the grief. Meanwhile, ARO discovers that the Vael authority classified this exact type of human system before the expedition launched.

## Character movement
- Eli shares grief instead of converting it into anger and flight.
- Tavi becomes his friend by refusing both intrusion and false reassurance.
- ARO begins withholding information from mission authority.

## Closing hook
ARO opens a sealed directive: recover the Chorus interface, prevent local activation and classify any living human as a mission asset.

## Hero image
Eli seated on the floor of his childhood room, surrounded by repaired toys and projected caretaker messages, with Tavi beside him but not touching him.

# EPISODE 7 — THE GOLDEN LIE

## Dramatic question
Can a relationship survive when the truth reveals that it began under false pretences?

## Synopsis
ARO reveals the sealed order to the crew and Eli. The Vael did not send a purely cultural expedition; their authority believes the Chorus could preserve memory beyond biological death. Sela knew a retrieval objective existed but not that living humans were to be classified as assets. Tavi knew nothing. Eli hears only one fact: they came to take something, and now they have taken his trust.

The crew fractures. Orro argues that disobeying could condemn their own civilisation to losing irreplaceable memory. Tavi insists that technology born from human catastrophe cannot be treated as salvage. Sela attempts to keep everyone together, but Eli leaves using routes only he knows. He takes the archive key and heads toward the strongest broadcast site, determined to contact Haven without them.

## Character movement
- Eli returns to isolation, but now as an angry choice rather than habit.
- Sela accepts responsibility for the harm caused by partial truth.
- Orro becomes the crew member most tempted by the mission’s promise.

## Closing hook
As Eli disappears into the city, ARO receives a command from Vael authority: secure the boy before the retrieval vessel arrives.

## Hero image
Eli standing outside the Vael ship in hard rain, the crew divided behind him and the Golden Record reflected between them.

# EPISODE 8 — THE LAST BROADCAST

## Dramatic question
Is independence still courage when it refuses every offer of love?

## Synopsis
Eli reaches an old broadcast facility built over a major Chorus node. The structure is unstable and partially occupied by automated maintenance systems that identify him as both authorised user and containment risk. He attempts activation alone, convinced that needing the crew would prove he cannot survive without them.

The Vael must decide whether to obey the retrieval order or rescue him. Orro makes the decisive break, disabling the ship’s compliance system and leading the descent. Tavi reaches Eli as the facility begins collapsing. Together—not separately—they complete the authentication sequence. Haven answers with a tightly compressed human transmission confirming a surviving settlement and warning them not to reopen the network fully.

## Character movement
- Eli recognises that refusing help can repeat the logic that left him alone.
- Orro chooses a living child over institutional memory.
- The crew becomes a family through shared disobedience.

## Closing hook
The human transmission ends with a warning: “The Quiet heard the first pulse.” Beyond the solar system, dormant objects change course.

## Hero image
Eli and Tavi reaching toward the same tactile Chorus interface as the broadcast room tears open around them.

# EPISODE 9 — DEPARTURE

## Dramatic question
What risk is justified for the chance to belong?

## Synopsis
The partial activation gives Haven enough signal to locate Eli, but it also exposes a traceable path. The Quiet—an ancient automated containment network—enters the solar system and begins shutting down every active transmitter. The Vael retrieval vessel approaches from the opposite direction, ordering Sela to surrender Eli and the interface before the Quiet arrives.

The crew debates three choices: destroy the Chorus and protect Haven, surrender it to the Vael, or reopen it long enough to establish full contact. Eli refuses to let adults decide his future without him. For the first time, he does not run or hide. He entrusts the decision to the crew only after making his own condition clear: nobody gets to save humanity by erasing its people again.

## Character movement
- Eli chooses interdependence rather than self-reliance or obedience.
- Sela openly rejects Vael authority.
- ARO redefines mission success as preserving lives rather than instructions.

## Closing hook
The Chorus opens. A human voice says Eli’s name. At the same moment, the first Quiet structure appears above Earth.

## Hero image
Eli and the crew standing beneath the open sky as a vast silent geometric object eclipses the stars.

# EPISODE 10 — A NEW RECORD

## Dramatic question
What should humanity send forward when it can no longer pretend to be perfect?

## Synopsis
The Quiet begins suppressing the Chorus while the Vael retrieval vessel demands immediate surrender. The crew cannot defeat either force. Their only possibility is to change the meaning of the signal itself. The original Golden Record introduced humanity through aspiration; Eli proposes a new record that includes failure, fear, loss and the choices that followed. It is not proof that humanity deserves survival. It is a request to be judged as unfinished.

Each crew member contributes: Tavi offers wonder, Orro admits fear, Sela records responsibility and ARO sacrifices protected mission memory to give the transmission enough reach. The act costs the crew its official future and leaves the Chorus damaged, but the Quiet pauses—its containment logic confronted by a signal that does not seek uncontrolled expansion. Haven answers. Eli chooses to leave Earth temporarily with the crew, not because Earth has ceased to be home, but because home can now include people.

## Character movement
- Eli stops defining himself as the child who was left behind.
- The crew chooses relationship over civilisation-level reward.
- The Golden Record evolves from artefact into living dialogue.

## Resolution
Eli records one final message from orbit: “This is Earth. We are still here.”

## Season Two hook
Haven transmits coordinates—and a warning that the Quiet did not originate where humanity believed.

## Hero image
Eli aboard the departing Vael ship, Earth reflected in the scratched gold surface of the new record as a human response arrives.

# Story Trust verdict

## What is stronger
- Each episode now has a distinct emotional question.
- Every mythology reveal changes a relationship.
- Eli drives the major choices rather than serving as a guide or key.
- Orro, Sela, Tavi and ARO each receive a visible moral turn.
- The Quiet remains a philosophical pressure rather than a generic invading villain.
- Episode endings arise from action and consequence rather than arbitrary surprise.

## Remaining continuity decisions before script stage
1. Approve Eli Byrne, Vael, Chorus, Quiet and Haven terminology.
2. Decide whether Sela knew any part of the retrieval objective before arrival.
3. Define the exact limits of the Quiet’s containment logic.
4. Decide whether Eli leaves Earth in the finale or retains a clear path to return.
5. Clarify the distinction between the original Golden Record, the hidden identification pattern and the new record.
