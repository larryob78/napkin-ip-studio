# THE LAST BOY ON EARTH
## Episodes 7–10 Full Dramatic Treatments

**Status:** Development draft v0.1  
**Format:** 10 episodes × approximately 15 minutes  
**Canon rule:** This document expands the approved season map using the recommended Memory Ark mythology. The Memory Ark remains a development recommendation pending founder lock. Names, exact ages, precise technical language, the identity of the boy’s carers and the final design of the approaching force remain provisional.

# EPISODE 7 — THE GOLDEN LIE

## Episode purpose

The season’s trust story breaks open. The boy discovers that the expedition came to Earth under false pretences, while the crew learns that their own civilisation has treated human memory as property. Every relationship is forced to choose between loyalty to authority and loyalty to one another.

## Human truth

A beautiful story can still become a lie when someone controls who is allowed to tell it.

## Opening image

Inside the boy’s former home, dawn light falls across the Golden Record, the expedition’s sealed mission file and a child’s hand-drawn map. Three ways of remembering the world sit beside one another, but only one has been honest about its incompleteness.

## Sequence 1 — The sealed order

The ship intelligence finishes decrypting the mission fragment detected in the hidden home systems. The expedition was not sent only to investigate the civilisation behind the Golden Record. Its primary classified objective was to locate and retrieve a terrestrial memory node before any competing power could reach it.

The order describes the node in strategic language: cultural reconstruction capacity, population cohesion, historical authentication. It never describes what the system means to the people whose memories it contains.

The leader initially argues that retrieval may still be protective. Their authority could secure the node and prevent misuse. The security officer points out that the crew was deliberately denied enough information to consent to the mission.

The young scientist looks at the boy and realises that every question they asked him was gathered inside a mission built on concealment.

**Character movement:** The leader’s faith in institutional purpose begins to separate from faith in the institution itself.

## Sequence 2 — The boy reads the omission

The boy does not understand every technical term, but he understands ownership. He asks the simplest question: if the expedition came because of humanity’s message, why did they arrive intending to take something without asking anyone?

No one can answer cleanly.

The boy connects the hidden order to the archive, the protected home and the adults who decided what he should know. He sees a pattern of authorities making choices about his life while calling it protection.

The young scientist apologises, but he rejects the apology because it comes before action. He accuses the crew of loving the Golden Record because it presents humanity as a beautiful object that cannot disagree with them.

**Emotional turn:** The season’s central friendship fractures not because the scientist lied personally, but because affection has not yet become moral courage.

## Sequence 3 — The Memory Ark fragment

The hidden systems activate a limited visual record. It does not reveal a giant machine. It reveals a distributed human project assembled across schools, hospitals, libraries, homes and civic networks.

The crew sees fragments of languages, songs, oral histories, recipes, migration records, scientific knowledge and contradictory accounts of the same events. The system’s purpose was not to create one perfect history. It was to help separated communities remain capable of arguing with, correcting and recognising one another.

A recorded explanation states that no archive can decide by itself what a civilisation was. A living witness must remain able to question it.

The boy stops the playback before the identity of that witness or the full plan is revealed.

**Mythology reveal:** The hidden technology is a cultural continuity system, not a weapon, resurrection machine or artificial person.

## Sequence 4 — The command demand

Alien authority detects the crew’s access and sends an immediate retrieval command. The message is formal, calm and framed as emergency stewardship. It orders the leader to secure the node, isolate the human survivor and avoid unauthorised transmission.

The phrase “human survivor” confirms that command knew a person might still be present.

The security officer wants to sever contact. The leader delays, still hoping for clarification. The young scientist openly challenges the leader: every minute of delay is another decision made over the boy’s head.

The boy hears enough. He takes his map, a compact human transmitter and the small keepsake recovered earlier in the season. He leaves through a route only he knows.

## Sequence 5 — The choice not made

The crew follows but reaches a split in the hidden route network. Their scanners fail inside the old civic systems. The leader must decide whether to obey the retrieval order, protect the expedition or search for the boy.

For several damaging seconds, the leader does not choose.

That hesitation is the episode’s betrayal.

The boy, watching unseen from a maintenance level, sees it.

He continues alone toward the broadcast site indicated by the archive route.

## Ending image

The Golden Record plays aboard the silent ship while the boy moves through the dark city below. The music once represented invitation. Now it sounds like evidence in a case about who gets to speak for humanity.

## Episode consequences

- The crew’s true retrieval mission is exposed.
- Alien authority knew a human survivor might be connected to the Earth node.
- The Memory Ark’s cultural purpose is established without locking its complete mechanism.
- The boy and young scientist’s relationship breaks.
- The leader’s hesitation costs the boy’s trust.
- The boy leaves the crew and heads toward the final broadcast facility.

## Canon protection

This treatment does not lock the authority’s ultimate policy, the carers’ identities, the Ark’s exact technical architecture or whether the boy was formally designated as its sole witness.

# EPISODE 8 — THE LAST BROADCAST

## Episode purpose

The boy attempts to reclaim agency by acting alone, while the divided crew must decide whether protecting him matters more than preserving mission legitimacy. The existence of off-world human settlements becomes undeniable.

## Human truth

Doing everything alone can feel like freedom when dependence has become dangerous.

## Opening image

Before sunrise, the boy crosses a flooded broadcast district in a small improvised boat. Transmission towers rise from the water like dead trees. Above him, the alien ship remains distant and unlit.

## Sequence 1 — The facility at the edge of the city

The boy reaches a former national broadcast complex linked to underground civic infrastructure. The public-facing studios are ruined, but protected transmission systems remain beneath them.

He navigates using lessons remembered from the home network rather than full technical knowledge. His competence is real but incomplete. He can wake the system, but not predict every consequence.

The facility responds to ordinary human actions: spoken phrases, physical switches, familiar symbols and a sequence embedded in the hand-drawn map. The technology feels inherited and maintained, not magical.

**Visual opportunity:** a child-sized figure in a vast dark studio as thousands of dormant indicator lights wake around him.

## Sequence 2 — The crew divides

Aboard the ship, command orders immediate departure with any recoverable mission data. The leader argues that the boy has become part of their duty of care. The security officer states the truth more plainly: leaving him would be abandonment.

The young scientist insists on going after him, even if that ends their career and severs their standing with home authority.

The leader finally chooses the boy over the mission, but the timing matters. The scientist does not offer forgiveness. They prepare a rescue because it is right, not because the relationship is repaired.

**Character turn:** The crew begins acting as a moral unit separate from command.

## Sequence 3 — The broken constellation

The boy activates a partial Memory Ark interface. A star map appears, but instead of nations or borders it shows scattered human settlements as incomplete constellations. Each carries different fragments of the archive.

One settlement preserved languages but lost much of Earth’s ecological record. Another retained science and engineering but only fragments of domestic culture. Another carries music, family histories and ritual, but no reliable account of why the migrations separated.

The settlements are not waiting as one united civilisation. They are isolated, culturally diverging and uncertain whether the others still exist.

A damaged message addresses the terrestrial custodian. It asks for authentication from Earth so the fragments can recognise and reconnect with one another.

**Major reveal:** Humanity survived beyond Earth, but not as a complete or unified society.

## Sequence 4 — The erasures

As the boy explores the map, several regions remain unnaturally blank. The system shows interrupted settlements whose histories end not with destruction footage but with replacement: names changed, records standardised, local voices removed.

A repeated symbol appears at the edge of multiple erased zones. It matches a pattern the alien crew detected entering the outer system.

The approaching force is introduced through absence rather than exposition. It conquers by controlling cultural continuity, making populations dependent on an approved version of themselves.

The boy realises the Earth node is not merely valuable because it contains information. It can help the settlements prove which records are their own.

## Sequence 5 — The rescue becomes consent

The crew reaches the facility as automated defences and structural failures threaten the boy. The security officer physically protects him while the leader disables the ship’s retrieval beacon, an act that openly defies command.

The young scientist reaches the Ark interface but does not touch it. They ask the boy what he wants to do.

It is the first time an authority figure in the season pauses before making the decision for him.

He says he wants to hear the full message before choosing.

The scientist helps stabilise the system under his direction.

**Relationship movement:** Trust does not return, but a new basis for it becomes possible: consent before protection.

## Sequence 6 — A human answer without a voice

The facility receives a narrow-band acknowledgement from one off-world settlement. It is not clear speech. It is a simple pattern encoded through a human song fragment also present on the Golden Record.

The signal proves someone is actively listening.

At the same moment, the approaching force changes course toward Earth.

## Ending image

Across the dark facility, isolated points on the star map pulse one by one. The boy stands at the centre of a broken human constellation, no longer certain he is alone but newly aware of the danger in being found.

## Episode consequences

- Off-world human settlements are confirmed.
- The Memory Ark is revealed as a distributed, incomplete system.
- The approaching force’s method of cultural erasure is established visually.
- The leader openly defies retrieval command.
- The security officer chooses the boy over mission preservation.
- The young scientist begins rebuilding trust by asking rather than deciding.
- A living settlement acknowledges Earth.

## Canon protection

This treatment does not lock the number, location, politics or identities of the human settlements, nor the name, species or complete history of the approaching force.

# EPISODE 9 — DEPARTURE

## Episode purpose

Hope becomes a decision with consequences. The boy and crew must choose between keeping humanity’s fragments hidden and reconnecting them at the risk of detection. The final conflict is framed as a moral and relational choice, not a battle for possession of a superweapon.

## Human truth

Safety can preserve life, but connection is what makes survival worth protecting.

## Opening image

The ship rises above Dublin carrying the boy and a portable interface linked to the Earth node. Below, the reclaimed city glows with scattered systems awakened by the broadcast. Far beyond the Moon, silent shapes enter the solar system.

## Sequence 1 — Three bad choices

The crew identifies three options:

1. leave the Earth node dormant, preserving secrecy but keeping human settlements divided
2. retrieve the node for alien authority, allowing one civilisation to control access
3. activate a reconnection process, helping the settlements recognise one another while revealing the network’s existence

No option is safe.

The leader finally explains the stakes to the boy without softening them. The boy asks each crew member what they want, reversing the season’s earlier pattern of adults questioning him.

The scientist wants reconnection but fears making humanity visible. The security officer prioritises immediate survival. The leader admits that returning the node to authority would preserve their career, civilisation’s advantage and perhaps the technology itself, but would repeat the theft at the heart of the mission.

## Sequence 2 — What the boy was taught

A final domestic recording from the protected home becomes accessible. The speaker’s identity remains obscured, but the lesson is clear: the boy was not left with a command to activate or guard the system forever. He was taught to ask whether people were ready to listen to one another without demanding ownership.

The recording says that preserving every memory is impossible. The task is to preserve the right of people to answer, correct and add to the record.

The boy understands that his role is not to unlock a machine through biology or destiny. It is to contribute living judgement where a system cannot.

**Character movement:** He stops defining himself as the object of other people’s plans.

## Sequence 3 — The pursuers speak

The approaching force sends a message offering safe passage in exchange for the Earth node. Its language is courteous and claims that central stewardship prevents conflict and misinformation.

The offer contains a demonstration: a recovered human story, altered subtly to present surrender as voluntary and inevitable.

The boy recognises an ordinary detail that has been removed. That small absence exposes the entire method.

The force does not need to destroy every culture. It only needs to become the authority that decides which version survives.

## Sequence 4 — The crew’s final division

Alien command re-establishes contact and orders the crew to accept protection, transfer the node and surrender the boy to specialist custody. The leader refuses.

A command override begins taking control of the ship. The crew must physically separate navigation, archive and communication systems. Their alien design culture, built around shared memory and transparent public record, becomes a dramatic advantage: they create a witnessed account of command’s actions and distribute it across the ship.

The security officer risks being trapped in a failing section to keep the override disconnected. The boy returns to help despite being offered a route to safety.

**Relationship consequence:** He chooses the crew before he knows whether they can escape, proving that belonging has begun to replace isolation.

## Sequence 5 — A fourth option

The boy rejects the assumption that they must transmit the entire Ark or keep it silent. He proposes creating a new record.

The new transmission will contain:

- a method for human settlements to authenticate one another without surrendering their archives
- selected cultural fragments offered with context rather than claimed as a definitive history
- testimony from the boy as a living person on Earth
- evidence of the approaching force’s alterations
- an invitation for every recipient to answer with its own record

The Ark remains distributed. No single authority receives the complete node.

The plan is technically difficult and still dangerous, but it changes the moral shape of the choice from possession to participation.

## Sequence 6 — Leaving Earth

The crew cannot remain on the surface. The boy has time to choose only a few objects to carry. He refuses grand monuments and chooses small things attached to lived memory: a recording, a repaired item from home, the map and one ordinary object gathered during the journey.

The Golden Record comes with them, not as the final word on humanity but as the first message in a continuing exchange.

## Ending image

The ship leaves Earth orbit while the unfinished new record assembles across its surfaces. Behind them, the approaching force passes the edge of the Moon. Ahead, scattered human settlements wait in silence.

## Episode consequences

- The boy’s role becomes moral witness rather than access key.
- Alien command attempts direct seizure and is rejected.
- The approaching force’s ideology is made explicit through altered memory.
- The crew becomes a chosen family through shared risk.
- The new-record strategy replaces the false binary between silence and surrender.
- The group leaves Earth with the transmission incomplete.

## Canon protection

This treatment does not lock the final object selection, exact pursuer design, the technical authentication method or whether alien public opinion immediately receives the crew’s evidence.

# EPISODE 10 — A NEW RECORD

## Episode purpose

The finale resolves the boy’s emotional isolation without pretending the wider danger is solved. The crew completes and transmits a new record of Earth, transforming the Golden Record’s one-way greeting into an open conversation.

## Human truth

Legacy is not the story we freeze forever. It is the invitation we leave for someone else to answer.

## Opening image

The alien ship moves through the shadow of Earth. Inside, the boy sits before the original Golden Record while fragments of the new transmission surround him: alien testimony, human recordings, maps, ordinary objects and empty spaces waiting for replies.

## Sequence 1 — What belongs in the record

The crew debates what to include. Their first attempts repeat the mistake of the original mission: they choose only humanity’s most impressive, beautiful or universal material.

The boy challenges each selection. A song means little without who sang it. A monument means little without who built, used or rejected it. A scientific achievement cannot stand without its consequences.

The new record begins to pair every grand fragment with a small lived context:

- a map beside a child’s route through one city
- a famous piece of music beside an imperfect domestic recording
- human engineering beside the reclaimed ecosystem that outlived it
- official history beside people who remembered it differently

The aliens add their own account of what they misunderstood and what they chose to do.

## Sequence 2 — The cost of sending

To transmit strongly enough for the settlements to authenticate one another, the ship must expose its position and sacrifice most of its remaining power. Escape becomes uncertain.

The security officer proposes transmitting only the technical handshake. The scientist argues that a code without testimony could be captured and controlled. The leader puts the final decision to the boy but makes clear that the consequence belongs to all of them.

The boy refuses to decide alone. Each crew member must choose publicly, creating the shared witness their culture values.

One by one, they consent.

## Sequence 3 — The pursuers arrive

The approaching force enters transmission range and begins erasing channels around them. Instead of a conventional weapons battle, the climax becomes a struggle to keep multiple voices alive long enough to be heard.

The crew fragments the new record across frequencies, ship systems and the original Golden Record’s recognised patterns. The force can suppress a central message, but not the many partial invitations spreading outward.

Alien command offers one final chance to return to protection. The leader broadcasts the sealed orders and the crew’s witnessed refusal instead.

The act turns private disobedience into public record.

## Sequence 4 — The boy’s testimony

The final missing element is the boy’s own message.

He initially tries to speak for all humanity and cannot. The scale paralyses him. The young scientist tells him he does not have to explain an entire species.

He speaks only as himself.

He says that Earth was full of people who loved badly, built beautifully, harmed one another, repaired things, forgot things and kept trying to be heard. He says he does not know whether humanity deserves to continue as one story. He knows no one else should own the answer.

He invites anyone listening to send back what they remember.

This testimony is not polished. Its pauses and uncertainty are what make it credible.

## Sequence 5 — Transmission

The crew initiates the new record.

Across the visual field, the broken constellation of settlements begins receiving different fragments. Some channels vanish under interference. Others split and continue. The Earth node remains behind, dormant but no longer isolated, while the authentication method travels outward.

The ship loses primary power and falls into darkness.

For several seconds there is no answer.

## Sequence 6 — The human voice

A faint transmission reaches the powerless ship. At first it sounds like noise. Then a human voice speaks.

The message is brief, damaged and not fully identifiable. It does not resolve where the speaker is, how many humans survived or whether rescue is possible.

The voice addresses the new record, not the original Golden Record.

It says, in effect: we heard you; we have something to send back.

The boy laughs and cries at once. The crew does not celebrate as observers. They gather around him as family.

## Resolution

Emergency systems return. The ship is damaged, exposed and cut off from command. Earth recedes behind them. The boy chooses to leave not because Earth no longer matters, but because carrying its stories into relationship is now more faithful than guarding them alone.

The young scientist returns the small keepsake he took when leaving the crew in Episode 7. No speech is required. Trust is not restored to innocence; it has been rebuilt through choice.

## Final image

The original Golden Record floats in the foreground of the ship’s archive. Beside it is a new, unfinished record with space deliberately left open. Beyond the window, one distant human signal becomes two, then three.

## Season-two launch point

The first responding settlement warns that the force approaching Earth has already altered human communities elsewhere. It also suggests that one surviving human group knows why the migrations separated and why the Earth witness was protected.

The new family sets a course toward the nearest reply.

## Episode consequences

- The crew permanently rejects its retrieval mission.
- The new record is transmitted as a distributed invitation rather than a definitive archive.
- The boy speaks as a living witness without becoming a chosen-one mechanism.
- Human survival is confirmed through an active reply.
- The boy’s isolation resolves into belonging.
- The larger conflict over memory, identity and cultural ownership opens Season Two.

## Canon protection

The responding speaker, settlement location, exact wording, pursuer identity and long-term status of the Earth node remain open for founder and production approval.

# FINAL-ACT CHARACTER ARCS

## The boy

He begins Episode 7 as someone whose life has repeatedly been managed through secrecy. He leaves to regain control, then discovers that total independence repeats his isolation. By the finale, he does not surrender agency to a new family. He learns to make shared decisions with people who ask for his consent and accept consequences beside him.

## The young scientist

They move from empathy without power to active moral courage. Their apology fails because it asks emotion to repair institutional harm. Trust begins to return only when they stop touching, interpreting and deciding without permission.

## The expedition leader

The leader’s arc is the cost of delayed courage. They first defend the possibility that authority may have good reasons, then hesitate when the boy leaves. Their final growth is public refusal: sacrificing career, mission legitimacy and institutional protection to create an honest record of what happened.

## The security officer

The security officer begins as the strongest voice for procedure and mission safety. By Episode 8, protection becomes personal rather than abstract. Their final loyalty is not sentimental; it is a reasoned conclusion that a mission which requires abandoning the child has already failed.

## Alien authority

Authority should remain credible enough to be dangerous. It believes central control can prevent misuse and cultural conflict. Its moral failure is treating stewardship as a right to possess, exclude and decide without the people whose history is at stake.

## The approaching force

The force is defined initially through erased differences, replacement histories and offers of orderly protection. It should not be reduced to destruction for its own sake. Its threat is a universe in which cultures survive physically but lose the ability to recognise or argue with their own past.

# CONTINUITY AND QUALITY CHECK

- Every final-act reveal changes a relationship and creates a later consequence.
- The Memory Ark is never presented as omniscient, conscious, magical or capable of resurrecting people.
- The boy’s importance comes from lived context and moral judgement, not biology or inherited access.
- The approaching force is introduced through cultural erasure before physical confrontation.
- The crew’s break with authority develops across hesitation, rescue, public refusal and shared sacrifice.
- The Golden Record remains central by evolving from one-way greeting into the model for an answerable, unfinished record.
- The finale resolves the boy’s isolation while preserving the wider mystery.
- Earth remains emotionally central even after the characters leave it.
