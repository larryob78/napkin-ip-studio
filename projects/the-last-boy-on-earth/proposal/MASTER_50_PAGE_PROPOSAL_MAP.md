# The Last Boy on Earth
## Master 50-Page Series Development Proposal Map

**Status:** v0.1 assembly blueprint  
**Purpose:** single canonical sequence for the Greenlight Book, PowerPoint and PDF  
**Format target:** 50 landscape pages, predominantly full-bleed cinematic imagery with restrained editorial copy  
**Canon rule:** anything not locked in `CANON.md` remains labelled provisional or unresolved

---

## Editorial architecture

The proposal should read as one emotional argument, not a database of development notes:

1. **Invite the reader into the image and premise.**
2. **Make the friendship emotionally irresistible.**
3. **Reveal the world and mystery in controlled layers.**
4. **Prove the season can sustain ten episodes.**
5. **Demonstrate a distinctive visual and production system.**
6. **End with a precise greenlight ask.**

Each page should perform one primary job. Avoid pages that simultaneously explain plot, theme, character, production and market strategy.

# Page-by-page sequence

## ACT I — THE INVITATION

### Page 01 — Cover
**Purpose:** immediate emotional and visual promise.  
**Copy:** title only, plus optional line: *The last human child meets the first visitor who came looking for us.*  
**Image:** the boy and the young alien scientist overlooking reclaimed Dublin at first light.  
**Source status:** image brief required; title and premise aligned with current canon.

### Page 02 — The Image That Started It
**Purpose:** introduce the Voyager Golden Record as the invitation that outlived humanity.  
**Copy:** 60–80 words on the record crossing deep time and being found.  
**Image:** Golden Record suspended against impossible interstellar darkness, small and human-made.  
**Source:** pitch document, canon, season map.

### Page 03 — One-Line Premise
**Purpose:** crystal-clear commercial comprehension.  
**Copy:** one-line premise, logline and format strip.  
**Image:** alien craft entering cloud above a green, silent Earth.  
**Format strip:** 10 episodes × approximately 15 minutes; premium family science-fiction adventure.

### Page 04 — Series Overview
**Purpose:** explain the complete dramatic engine in under 180 words.  
**Copy:** arrival, discovery of the boy, friendship, concealed mission, approaching threat, new Golden Record.  
**Image:** wide encounter composition with emotional distance between the crew and the boy.

### Page 05 — Why This Story
**Purpose:** state the human question beneath the science fiction.  
**Core thought:** being remembered is not the same as being known.  
**Image:** the Golden Record reflected in the boy’s eye or held near an alien hand.

### Page 06 — The Emotional Promise
**Purpose:** define the audience experience.  
**Copy pillars:** wonder, loneliness, humour, suspense, belonging, hope.  
**Image:** the two children beneath a night sky, separated by species but sharing one listening device.

## ACT II — THE WORLD WE LEFT BEHIND

### Page 07 — Earth Is Alive
**Purpose:** reject the generic dead-apocalypse shorthand.  
**Copy:** Earth has recovered ecologically while human systems have fallen quiet.  
**Image:** sweeping reclaimed-Dublin aerial, beautiful rather than ruin-porn.  
**Source:** `RECLAIMED_EARTH_WORLD_BIBLE.md`.

### Page 08 — Reclaimed Dublin
**Purpose:** establish the city as a character.  
**Copy:** recognisable civic geometry, wet light, vegetation, river, stone, glass and silence.  
**Image:** River Liffey and bridges transformed by decades of ecological return.

### Page 09 — Four Location Families
**Purpose:** prove visual and production repeatability.  
**Copy:** Vertical City, River City, Memory Interiors, Hidden Systems.  
**Image:** four-frame cinematic location plate, not a technical diagram.

### Page 10 — The Vertical City
**Purpose:** establish scale, climbing and danger.  
**Image:** the boy crossing upper levels of a vegetated civic structure above the streets.  
**Story function:** survival knowledge becomes character authority.

### Page 11 — The River City
**Purpose:** introduce movement, reflection and changing weather.  
**Image:** shallow craft or improvised crossing through the Liffey corridor.  
**Story function:** spaces that open and conceal routes.

### Page 12 — Memory Interiors
**Purpose:** create intimacy and the ache of ordinary human absence.  
**Image:** preserved bedroom, library, museum or kitchen entered by alien visitors.  
**Rule:** detail should suggest interrupted lives, not horror tableaux.

### Page 13 — Hidden Systems
**Purpose:** seed the central mythology without explaining it.  
**Image:** dormant infrastructure waking below roots and flood marks.  
**Copy:** someone designed parts of Earth to remember, wait and respond.

### Page 14 — The Memory Ark
**Purpose:** present the recommended causal mythology as provisional development.  
**Copy:** a distributed cultural-continuity system preserving context, testimony and routes to off-world settlements.  
**Image:** layered human archive activating around the boy and scientist.  
**Label:** recommended framework, pending founder lock.

### Page 15 — The Golden Record and the New Record
**Purpose:** connect the historic object to the season climax.  
**Copy:** the original record speaks outward; the new record becomes answerable, plural and alive.  
**Image:** visual contrast between the single gold disc and a constellation of living transmissions.

## ACT III — THE PEOPLE WHO FIND EACH OTHER

### Page 16 — The Boy
**Purpose:** introduce the emotional centre without locking unsupported biography.  
**Copy:** observant, capable, funny when safe, suspicious of rescue, starved of equal companionship.  
**Image:** front-facing cinematic portrait in practical, repaired clothing.  
**Unresolved:** final name, exact age, family history, duration alone.

### Page 17 — Survival Is Character
**Purpose:** show how he has lived rather than merely stating that he survived.  
**Copy:** route memory, weather reading, salvaged systems, cautious rituals, attachment to objects and places.  
**Image:** the boy moving through a daily survival routine that feels practised, not heroicised.

### Page 18 — The Young Scientist
**Purpose:** introduce the alien child/young scientist as co-lead.  
**Copy:** curious, brilliant, emotionally direct, raised within a culture that treats memory as civic responsibility.  
**Image:** intimate 85mm portrait with humanity in the eyes and restrained alien design.

### Page 19 — The Expedition Leader
**Purpose:** establish adult authority under moral pressure.  
**Copy:** trained to protect the mission, forced to decide whether legitimacy matters more than the child in front of them.  
**Image:** command portrait with the sealed mission visible only as subtext.

### Page 20 — The Crew
**Purpose:** prove ensemble contrast and humour.  
**Copy:** concise role cards for leader, scientist, systems specialist and cultural interpreter.  
**Image:** group in ship interior, each silhouette and behaviour distinct.  
**Canon note:** retain provisional labels until names are locked.

### Page 21 — The Friendship Engine
**Purpose:** articulate the repeatable heart of every episode.  
**Copy:** each child knows something the other cannot learn alone; trust grows through exchange, error and consent.  
**Image:** the boy teaching an Earth skill while the scientist shares alien listening technology.

### Page 22 — Relationship Map
**Purpose:** show changing loyalties rather than static descriptions.  
**Copy:** boy ↔ scientist; boy ↔ leader; scientist ↔ command; crew ↔ mission; all ↔ absent humanity.  
**Image:** editorial relationship composition using portraits and negative space, not corporate arrows.

## ACT V — VISUAL IDENTITY

### Page 39 — Visual Thesis
**Purpose:** state the art-direction promise.  
**Copy:** premium live-action credibility, child-scale intimacy, real weather, restrained alien technology, beauty without sentimentality.  
**Image:** definitive series hero still.

### Page 40 — Camera Language
**Purpose:** establish repeatable cinematography.  
**Copy:** low child-height camera, 35–85mm emotional range, patient wides, natural lens behaviour, motivated movement.  
**Source:** `ART-DIRECTION-SHOT-BIBLE.md`.

### Page 41 — Light and Colour
**Purpose:** define progression across the season.  
**Copy:** wet greens and slate blues; warm human interiors; alien whites and mineral tones; gold reserved for memory, invitation and choice.  
**Image:** colour-script strip across ten episodes.

### Page 42 — Alien Design Grammar
**Purpose:** prove originality and consistency.  
**Copy:** anatomy, gesture, textiles, tools and interfaces derived from a culture of shared memory.  
**Image:** matched portrait, silhouette, hand/tool and material studies.  
**Source:** `ALIEN-CULTURE-DESIGN-GRAMMAR.md`.

### Page 43 — The Ship
**Purpose:** establish a non-cliché vehicle and production anchor.  
**Copy:** object shaped by listening, preservation and long-duration travel rather than aggression.  
**Image:** exterior and one emotionally legible interior.

### Page 44 — Technology and Interfaces
**Purpose:** set rules against generic floating holograms.  
**Copy:** physical consequence, layered memory, touch and permission, light as response rather than decoration.  
**Image:** child interacting with a tactile archive interface.

### Page 45 — Full-Bleed Image Production System
**Purpose:** show how the complete book will be produced consistently.  
**Copy:** canon read → image brief → generation → critique → continuity lock → export.  
**Include:** naming scheme, aspect ratio, safe copy zones and approved/rejected folders.  
**Source:** Greenlight Image Director skill.

### Page 46 — Priority Image Sequence
**Purpose:** define the first twelve images needed for a credible greenlight review.  
**Sequence:** cover; Golden Record; arrival; first meeting; Earth aerial; boy portrait; scientist portrait; group; archive; home; sealed mission; new record.  
**Output:** numbered production checklist with status fields.

## ACT VI — PRODUCTION AND GREENLIGHT

### Page 47 — Two-Minute Proof
**Purpose:** define the smallest test that proves the project.  
**Content:** arrival over Dublin, first encounter, one comic misunderstanding, one intimate exchange, Golden Record reveal.  
**Deliverables:** script, numbered shot list, character continuity pack, sound and voice test.

### Page 48 — AI-Enabled Production Approach
**Purpose:** present the production method without letting technology overshadow story.  
**Copy:** controlled visual development, character consistency, virtual production, motion tests, voice prototyping, editorial iteration and human creative approval at every gate.

### Page 49 — Series and Franchise Potential
**Purpose:** show expansion without diluting Season One.  
**Copy:** other human settlements, deeper alien political conflict, cultural recovery, new records, family and youth audience extensions.  
**Guardrail:** Season One remains emotionally complete around the boy’s first answered voice.

### Page 50 — The Ask
**Purpose:** finish with a simple decision.  
**Copy:** approve development of a two-minute proof and align on funding, distribution, production route and IP participation.  
**Image:** the boy and scientist listening to the returned human voice, facing forward into the next chapter.

# Continuity guardrails for assembly

- Do not assign a final name or exact age to the boy until approved.
- Do not state a definitive cause for humanity’s disappearance.
- Do not make the boy a biological key, chosen one or sole representative of humanity.
- Do not present the Memory Ark recommendation as locked canon.
- Keep the alien expedition’s sealed mission morally consequential but understandable.
- Preserve the final answer from a living human voice.
- Keep Dublin recognisable, specific and ecologically alive.
- Keep technology physical, purposeful and culturally derived.
- Every episode page must show a relationship change and a later consequence.
