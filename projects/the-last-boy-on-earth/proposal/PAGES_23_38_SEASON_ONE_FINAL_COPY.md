# PAGE 23 — SEASON ONE
## Ten chapters. One growing act of trust.

Season One begins with an impossible arrival and ends with an answer humanity could never have sent from the Voyager spacecraft: a living voice.

Across ten approximately 15-minute episodes, the story expands in three movements.

**CONTACT**  
An alien expedition follows the Golden Record to a living but silent Earth. They discover a single boy, learn that survival has made him wary rather than helpless, and begin to understand humanity through the ordinary things it left behind.

**THE SIGNAL**  
The boy leads the crew through a reclaimed Dublin that he knows better than any scanner. A repeating human transmission, a protected archive and the place he once called home reveal that his survival may not have been accidental.

**THE CHOICE**  
A concealed alien mission turns friendship into betrayal. Evidence of surviving human communities beyond Earth changes the meaning of the boy's loneliness, while an approaching force makes memory itself dangerous. The season ends not with possession of a lost civilisation, but with a choice about who has the right to speak for it.

**Season movement:** Arrival → Contact → Exploration → Signal → Archive → Home → Betrayal → Broadcast → Choice → Answer.

**Image direction:** ten-image cinematic ribbon progressing from vast cosmic isolation to the intimate final act of transmission.

# PAGE 24 — EPISODES 1–3
## CONTACT

### Episode 1 — Working title: **The Signal**
An alien expedition reaches Earth after following the legacy of the Voyager Golden Record. They expect cities, transmissions and a civilisation capable of answering its own invitation. Instead they find a breathtaking world reclaimed by nature and evidence that one person is still moving through the ruins. Their first encounter with the boy becomes a tense pursuit until a crew member chooses to save him rather than mission equipment. He offers one tiny act of trust in return. That night, the crew discovers he already knows the music on the Golden Record.

### Episode 2 — Working title: **The Visitors**
First contact becomes something harder: coexistence. The crew tries to learn about the boy through questions and technology, but he responds to behaviour instead. A failed introduction, a shared meal and an exchange of personal objects teach both sides that trust is built through boundaries, consent and small acts of respect. When the boy finally shares his hand-drawn map, alien scans reveal that several mysterious signals across Dublin have changed after he listened to them. Something on Earth may already know he is there.

### Episode 3 — Working title: **Museum of Everything**
The crew enters a vast museum transformed into an indoor ecosystem and repeatedly misreads the ordinary objects of human life. The comedy slowly becomes grief as the boy corrects their assumptions and shows that value cannot be measured by rarity alone. Inside a room of recorded human voices, the aliens encounter memory as messy, contradictory presence rather than organised data. Evidence that an intelligent presence has moved through the building recently changes the expedition's central question from “What happened here?” to “Who is still acting?”

**Image direction:** triptych. Descent over reclaimed Dublin. First face-to-face encounter. Alien child among ordinary human artefacts.

# PAGE 25 — EPISODE 1
## THE SIGNAL
### The world called. Someone answered.

Deep space. The Voyager Golden Record crosses darkness long after the civilisation that made it has fallen silent. An alien expedition has followed its legacy across an immense distance, expecting to meet the people who once placed greetings, music, laughter and instructions into the stars.

Earth is blue, green and alive. Its cities are dark.

On the ground, the crew finds tiny signs of recent human intention: a repaired rain collector, a cut rope, a sharpened tool, maps marked by a hand that appears to have grown older over time. Then someone steals one of their scanners.

The boy knows the city better than their instruments do. A pursuit through flooded, overgrown infrastructure ends in collapse. Faced with a choice between recovering equipment and saving a stranger, a crew member chooses the boy.

He does not join them. He does not explain himself. He simply returns the scanner.

That night the expedition analyses his reaction to music from the Golden Record. He did not merely recognise it. He anticipated it.

In his hidden shelter, a damaged human player repeats the same music beside a map covered in signal marks.

**Episode turn:** discovery becomes responsibility.  
**Cliffhanger:** why does the boy know a message sent into space centuries before?

**Image direction:** the boy in a flooded transit concourse, holding the alien scanner as both shield and question, warm record-light against cool reclaimed-city darkness.

# PAGE 26 — EPISODE 2
## THE VISITORS
### Trust begins with what you do not take.

The aliens return with gifts. They choose badly.

The boy ignores sophisticated medical and archival technology and notices instead the simple heat source they use to prepare food. Their translation system improves, but the young scientist asks too many questions and follows when he leaves. He responds with a harmless trap and a very clear lesson: curiosity does not erase boundaries.

Later, a failed attempt to cook local food brings them together. The boy helps. The crew imitates him badly. The ship intelligence briefly classifies laughter as distress. For the first time, he eats in front of other people.

When the scientist reaches toward a worn object attached to his bag, the boy recoils. The scientist returns it without scanning and places an alien memory token beside it as collateral. He understands that the object matters and returns the token untouched.

Only then does he show them his map.

Alien orbital data confirms that several of his signal marks are real. Stranger still, one transmission appears to have changed timing after he listened.

Something may have responded to him.

**Episode turn:** observation becomes relationship.  
**Cliffhanger:** a new mark appears on the boy's map overnight.

**Image direction:** shared meal beneath a collapsed glass roof, awkward alien visitors and one guarded child beginning, almost accidentally, to laugh together.

# PAGE 27 — EPISODE 3
## MUSEUM OF EVERYTHING
### Objects survive. Meaning has to be carried.

A mysterious signal leads the crew toward a museum the boy does not want to enter.

Inside, trees grow through exhibition halls and animals nest among preserved artefacts. The aliens catalogue human objects with complete confidence and spectacularly wrong conclusions: a pushchair becomes a shrine, a mascot costume evidence of political leadership, a toy an instrument of unknown ceremonial importance.

The boy cannot help correcting them.

Their mistakes open the door to something deeper. During a structural collapse, the crew prioritises technical material while the boy risks himself for a small ordinary object. It has almost no historical value, but somebody once held it when they were afraid. The aliens begin to understand that what survives is not the same as what matters.

In a damaged installation, recorded human voices overlap through darkness: jokes, testimony, family stories, unfinished thoughts. The aliens stop analysing and simply listen.

Then they find physical evidence that the museum has been entered recently by an intelligence that is neither ordinary decay nor the boy.

**Episode turn:** archaeology becomes active mystery.  
**Question carried forward:** if the boy is alone, who else has been moving through the city?

**Image direction:** young alien scientist and boy standing among thousands of suspended human voice sources in a dark, overgrown gallery.

# PAGE 28 — EPISODES 4–6
## THE SIGNAL

### Episode 4 — **The City That Grew Back**
To reach a new relay, the crew trusts alien scanning over the boy's local knowledge. He proves that the shortest route is not the safest and becomes the expedition's guide through a city transformed into an ecosystem. When he is trapped during a structural collapse, the expedition leader abandons mission equipment to rescue him. At sunset, the boy repairs a dormant relay by hand. A recent human transmission emerges. Across Dublin, distant lights answer in a pattern nobody can yet explain.

### Episode 5 — **A Voice in the Dark**
Coordinates hidden inside the transmission lead beneath the city to a protected archive of ordinary human life: school recordings, family videos, recipes, jokes and unfinished conversations. The preservation is beautiful to the aliens and unbearable to the boy. Then a restricted system activates specifically when he enters and plays a damaged message addressed directly to him. He admits he has followed versions of the signal before and stopped because hope made surviving harder. The message reveals a route to a place he knows.

### Episode 6 — **Home**
The route leads to the boy's former home, hidden behind defences designed to look natural. It is not a bunker but a domestic survival network: water, food, lessons, repaired clothes and traces of people who planned for his future. The crew discovers that someone deliberately taught, protected and concealed him. A fragment of hidden mission data then reveals a disturbing connection: alien command knew enough about this place to keep information from the expedition itself.

**Image direction:** three-image sequence. Vertical green city. Protected archive beneath black water. Quiet domestic shelter reclaimed by woodland.

# PAGE 29 — EPISODE 4
## THE CITY THAT GREW BACK
### The city becomes his proof.

Alien maps identify a direct route to a new signal relay. The boy refuses it.

He reads what their sensors miss: nesting behaviour, changing water marks, stressed roots, the sound of structures settling under decades of weather. What looks inefficient to the crew is survival knowledge made physical.

As they cross rooftops, service bridges and buildings transformed into wetlands, the expedition's hierarchy quietly changes. The boy is no longer cargo, witness or mystery. He is the person who knows how to move through this world.

Then a living bridge of roots and old infrastructure tears loose. The boy falls into a flooded service chamber. Protocol says preserve the expedition. The leader goes after him anyway, sacrificing mission equipment to keep a failing structure open.

At the relay, the boy manually restores a connection that alien technology alone could not solve. A human phrase begins repeating across the city.

The signal is recent enough to matter.

Far away, lights answer.

**Episode turn:** the leader's duty becomes personal.  
**Mystery advance:** human systems are not merely surviving; some appear to have been maintained.

**Image direction:** tiny figures crossing a green canyon between Dublin buildings, scale enormous, route perilous, the boy unmistakably in front.

# PAGE 30 — EPISODE 5
## A VOICE IN THE DARK
### Hope is the dangerous thing.

The repeating transmission hides coordinates beneath its damaged language. They lead below Dublin.

The boy refuses to go, then joins because he cannot let the crew enter alone. Together they descend through flooded public ruins into infrastructure that has been intentionally protected from the world above.

The archive they find is not a vault of weapons or scientific secrets. It is an ocean of ordinary life: private recordings, local radio, school voices, domestic video, recipes, jokes. The aliens' culture reveres preserved memory. The boy knows that preserved memory can hurt.

Inside a restricted chamber, the system responds to his presence.

A human message begins.

Most of it is damaged. One fact survives intact: it is addressed to him.

He finally admits that he has heard fragments before. He followed them for years, and every trail ended in silence. Eventually he chose certainty over hope because hope made loneliness harder to survive.

The archive reveals a partial route to a place connected to his past.

He recognises a gesture inside the damaged recording.

“I know where we have to go.”

**Episode turn:** secrecy becomes understandable self-protection.  
**Midpoint reveal:** the boy's isolation may be part of a larger plan.

**Image direction:** child and young alien scientist in a vast archive chamber as an incomplete human face flickers into view.

# PAGE 31 — EPISODE 6
## HOME
### Someone planned for him to continue.

Beyond the centre of Dublin, the boy leads the crew into a quieter district where houses and gardens have merged into woodland.

The route to his former home is hidden by ordinary-looking obstacles: redirected water, thorn growth, false dead ends, collapsed walls. To alien sensors it appears accidental. To anyone who knows what to look for, it is care expressed as concealment.

Inside is a domestic survival network, not a fortress. Height marks climb a wall. Clothing has been repaired in several sizes. Different voices have recorded lessons. Water, food and power systems are designed to be understood and maintained by one growing child.

The place reframes everything the crew believed about him. He did not survive because he was superhuman, chosen by technology or uniquely immune to catastrophe. Someone prepared him to keep going.

The tenderness is interrupted by a hidden data exchange. The ship detects a fragment of its own mission architecture inside the human systems.

Alien command knew something about this place before the crew arrived.

The discovery makes the boy's home the point where two concealed plans meet.

**Episode turn:** survival becomes evidence of absent relationships.  
**Cliffhanger:** the expedition's mission was never as innocent as its crew believed.

**Image direction:** boy standing inside a quiet, preserved room with height marks and repaired clothing; alien scientist remains at the doorway, giving him space.

# PAGE 32 — EPISODES 7–10
## THE CHOICE

### Episode 7 — **The Golden Lie**
The crew decrypts its sealed mission objective: locate and retrieve a terrestrial memory node before another power reaches it. Alien command knew a human survivor might be present. The boy sees the contradiction immediately. The aliens loved humanity's Golden Record because it could not disagree with them, yet arrived intending to take part of humanity's memory without consent. Trust breaks. He leaves the crew and heads alone toward the final broadcast site.

### Episode 8 — **The Last Broadcast**
The boy activates part of the recommended Memory Ark framework and discovers scattered off-world human communities carrying incomplete fragments of their shared past. They are alive but disconnected. Several regions on the map show something more disturbing: histories that have been standardised, renamed or erased. The alien crew finally chooses the boy over command and asks what he wants before touching the system. A living human settlement answers Earth.

### Episode 9 — **Departure**
The crew faces three dangerous choices: keep Earth's memory hidden, surrender it to alien authority, or reconnect human communities and reveal the network to the approaching force. A fourth option emerges from the boy: do not transmit ownership of the archive. Create a new record, built from voices that can answer one another. As command attempts to seize the ship, the boy chooses to remain with the crew that has finally chosen him.

### Episode 10 — **A New Record**
The climax is an act of communication rather than conquest. The boy, the crew and scattered human communities begin assembling a distributed message no single civilisation can own. The approaching force can suppress a fixed archive; it cannot easily control a living network of people capable of correcting, contradicting and adding to the story. The season closes as Earth transmits not a perfect definition of humanity, but an invitation to answer.

**Image direction:** four-image progression from betrayal to broadcast to moral choice to constellation of living voices.

# PAGE 33 — EPISODE 7
## THE GOLDEN LIE
### Being remembered is not the same as being allowed to speak.

The sealed mission file finally opens.

The expedition did not cross the stars only to meet the civilisation behind the Golden Record. Its classified objective was to locate and retrieve a terrestrial memory node before a competing power could reach it.

Alien command describes culture in strategic language: authentication, reconstruction capacity, cohesion, control. The boy understands the moral problem faster than anyone else.

If they came because humanity sent a message, why did they arrive planning to take something without asking?

The crew has no clean answer.

A hidden human system reveals fragments of a distributed Memory Ark: schools, homes, libraries, hospitals and civic networks preserving contradictory versions of human life so no single archive could become the official truth.

Then command issues a direct order: secure the node, isolate the human survivor, prevent unauthorised transmission.

“Human survivor.”

They knew he might be here.

The boy leaves before the crew can decide what to do. The leader hesitates for only seconds between mission, crew and child.

For the boy, those seconds are enough.

**Episode turn:** affection without action is exposed as insufficient.  
**Season fracture:** the central friendship breaks.

**Image direction:** Golden Record, sealed alien mission file and the boy's hand-drawn map in one frame, three competing claims on memory.

# PAGE 34 — EPISODE 8
## THE LAST BROADCAST
### Humanity survived. Just not together.

Before sunrise, the boy reaches an abandoned broadcast complex linked to the hidden systems beneath Dublin. He can wake the machinery, but he cannot control everything that follows.

A star map emerges.

Humanity is not gone.

Scattered settlements survive beyond Earth, each carrying incomplete pieces of a larger inheritance. One community kept languages but lost much of Earth's ecology. Another preserved engineering but only fragments of domestic culture. Another carries music, ritual and family stories but cannot fully explain why the communities separated.

They are alive. They are divided. Some no longer know whether the others exist.

Then the map reveals blank regions where local histories appear to have been replaced by standardised accounts. An approaching force does not need to erase populations physically. It can erase the versions of themselves they are allowed to remember.

The alien crew arrives under orders to leave. Instead, the leader disables the retrieval beacon. The young scientist reaches the interface and stops.

“What do you want to do?”

For the first time, the decision belongs to the boy.

A distant human settlement answers Earth using a fragment of song.

The approaching force changes course.

**Episode turn:** the last boy may not be the last human.  
**Threat reveal:** memory itself is the contested territory.

**Image direction:** boy centred inside a dark broadcast studio as isolated human lights appear across an enormous broken constellation.

# PAGE 35 — EPISODE 9
## DEPARTURE
### Safety can keep you alive. Connection gives survival meaning.

The ship rises over Dublin while the crew faces three bad options.

Keep the Earth node dormant and human communities remain hidden but divided. Hand it to alien authority and one civilisation controls access to humanity's shared past. Reconnect the settlements and help them recognise one another, but reveal the network to the force already moving toward Earth.

A final domestic recording clarifies the boy's role. He was never designed as a biological key or chosen one. He was taught something a machine cannot decide: ask whether people are ready to listen without demanding ownership.

The approaching force offers “safe stewardship” in exchange for the archive, then demonstrates its method by presenting a subtly altered human story. One ordinary detail has been removed. The boy recognises the manipulation.

Alien command attempts to seize the ship remotely. The crew breaks its own systems apart to remain free.

Then the boy finds the fourth option.

Do not send the archive as an object.

Make a new record.

Let people answer it themselves.

**Episode turn:** the boy stops being the object of other people's plans and becomes an author of the next one.  
**Launch into finale:** the crew prepares a transmission that cannot belong to a single keeper.

**Image direction:** ship above reclaimed Dublin, boy between Earth and star map, with three routes collapsing into a fourth visual line.

# PAGE 36 — EPISODE 10
## A NEW RECORD
### The first record said: this is who we were. The new one says: answer us.

The finale rejects the idea that humanity can be saved by reducing it to one perfect archive.

The boy and the crew begin assembling a new Golden Record, not as a replacement disc but as a living transmission protocol. Human settlements contribute voices, contradictions, corrections, songs, domestic memories, arguments, scientific records and ordinary details. No contribution can become the final version of humanity because every message can be answered.

The approaching force can control a central object. It can rewrite a fixed story. A distributed conversation is harder to possess.

The crew uses its own culture's memory practices to witness alien command's actions and expose the retrieval mission. The boy contributes Earth not as a guardian speaking for everyone, but as one living participant among many.

The emotional climax is quiet.

The young scientist asks what the boy wants his first contribution to be.

Not a speech. Not an explanation of the catastrophe.

Something small, human and alive.

Earth transmits.

Across the dark, other lights begin to answer.

The season ends with the boy no longer alone and humanity no longer represented only by what it left behind.

**Season resolution:** memory becomes relationship.  
**Season Two door:** reconnection has begun, and so has the struggle over who gets to shape it.

**Image direction:** the boy and young scientist framed against a constellation of active transmissions, intimate faces foregrounded, cosmic scale behind them.

# PAGE 37 — CHARACTER ARCS
## Everyone arrives believing memory means something different.

### THE BOY
**Begins:** defended, competent and suspicious of rescue. He has survived by limiting dependence and treating hope as a risk.  
**Tested by:** visitors who care about humanity but repeatedly make decisions over his head.  
**Ends:** not “rescued,” but participating. He chooses connection while keeping the right to question, refuse and answer. His movement is from defended isolation to chosen belonging.

### THE YOUNG SCIENTIST
**Begins:** brilliant, curious and sincere, but assumes curiosity is inherently benevolent.  
**Tested by:** the discovery that observation, preservation and even affection can become forms of control without consent.  
**Ends:** with moral agency. The scientist stops asking only what can be known and begins asking who has the right to decide.

### THE EXPEDITION LEADER
**Begins:** convinced that ethical behaviour can exist inside legitimate protocol.  
**Tested by:** orders that call possession stewardship and delay responsibility in the language of procedure.  
**Ends:** choosing accountable protection over institutional permission, while accepting the cost of that choice.

### THE SECURITY OFFICER
**Begins:** most suspicious of Earth and most focused on practical risk.  
**Tested by:** a child whose vulnerability does not make him helpless and a mission whose authority does not make it right.  
**Ends:** as the crew member most willing to name abandonment and coercion plainly.

### THE CREW
**Begins:** observers of a lost civilisation.  
**Ends:** implicated witnesses inside a living one.

**Image direction:** four restrained character portraits with negative space between them gradually connected by shared memory objects, not graphic arrows.

# PAGE 38 — MYSTERY DISCIPLINE
## Answer enough to satisfy. Protect enough to continue.

The series mystery works only if every reveal changes a relationship and every answer creates a more precise question. Season One therefore resolves the emotional problem more fully than the historical one.

### REVEALED IN SEASON ONE
- Earth is ecologically alive while large-scale human civilisation is absent.
- The boy has been deliberately taught, supplied and concealed by human systems or people from his past.
- Alien command withheld material information from the expedition.
- The recommended Memory Ark framework is a distributed cultural-continuity system, not a weapon, resurrection device or biological key.
- Human communities survive beyond Earth in incomplete, separated forms.
- An approaching power has a pattern of standardising or erasing cultural continuity.
- The boy's value is his living judgement and right to answer, not unique biology.

### STRONGLY IMPLIED, NOT FULLY LOCKED
- The boy's survival and the Earth memory network are causally connected.
- The Golden Record and Earth's later signal history helped bring multiple powers toward the planet.
- The off-world communities have diverged enough that reconnection will create conflict as well as hope.
- Alien authority's motives may include genuine fear alongside strategic self-interest.

### HELD FOR LATER DEVELOPMENT
- The exact cause and timeline of humanity's disappearance from Earth.
- The boy's final name, exact age, carers and full biography.
- Whether any other humans remain physically on Earth.
- The number, location, cultures and politics of the off-world settlements.
- The identity, origin and complete agenda of the approaching force.
- The final technical language and architecture of the Memory Ark.
- The long-term consequences of the new record.

### CONTINUITY RULE
Episode titles in the 50-page proposal remain **working titles**. Earlier proposal-map labels such as “The Golden Record,” “The Last Boy” and “The Museum” should not override the detailed treatment titles “The Signal,” “The Visitors” and “Museum of Everything” without a deliberate editorial lock.

**Final thought:** Season One answers the question “Is he alone?” with something richer than yes or no. He is part of a human story that survived in fragments, and for the first time he gets to decide how he enters it.

**Image direction:** three layers of information fading from sharp foreground to unresolved distance: what we know, what we suspect, what still waits beyond the stars.
