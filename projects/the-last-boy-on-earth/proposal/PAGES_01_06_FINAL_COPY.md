## PAGE 02 — THE IMAGE THAT STARTED IT

### We sent a record into the dark.

In 1977, humanity launched a message beyond the Solar System: sounds, images, music, greetings and evidence that life on Earth had once looked outward and wondered who might be listening.

Long after the people who made it have vanished from Earth, that invitation is finally found.

An expedition follows it home expecting to meet a civilisation.

They find one child.

**Editorial note:** the historic Voyager Golden Record is the factual inspiration; the story’s future discovery and expedition are fictional premises.

**Image assignment**  
The Golden Record alone against immense interstellar darkness. It should feel handmade, finite and almost impossibly small. No spacecraft glamour. No explanatory graphics.

## PAGE 03 — THE PREMISE

### They crossed the stars to meet humanity. They arrived too late.

**One-line premise**  
An alien expedition follows humanity’s Voyager Golden Record to Earth and discovers that our civilisation has disappeared, leaving behind a single human boy.

**Series logline**  
When a young alien scientist befriends the last child living on a reclaimed Earth, their search for what happened to humanity exposes a secret inside the expedition itself and forces both children to decide whether the past should be preserved, obeyed or answered.

**Format**  
10 episodes × approximately 15 minutes  
Premium family science-fiction adventure  
Serialised mystery with an emotionally complete first-season arc

**Image assignment**  
Alien craft descending through cloud toward a silent, intensely green Earth. The vehicle should feel designed for listening and exploration, not war.

## PAGE 04 — SERIES OVERVIEW

### First contact happens after everyone is gone.

An expedition from a distant civilisation reaches Earth after discovering humanity’s Golden Record. They expect cities full of people. Instead they find a planet transformed by decades of ecological return and one human child who has learned to survive among the quiet remains.

The youngest member of the expedition, a brilliant and emotionally direct scientist, becomes fascinated by him. He is equally fascinated by someone who knows nothing about Earth and refuses to treat its ordinary objects as ordinary. Their friendship turns exploration into a two-way exchange: he teaches the visitors how to read the world that remains; they show him that humanity’s voice travelled farther than anyone imagined.

But the expedition did not come to Earth with the whole truth. As dormant systems wake, human signals begin to surface and the crew’s sealed mission collides with the children’s growing trust. The mystery stops being simply *Where did everyone go?* and becomes a harder question: *Who gets to decide what a civilisation is allowed to remember?*

**Image assignment**  
First wide encounter. Boy and crew separated by real physical distance. No weapons-forward staging. Curiosity and uncertainty carry the tension.

## PAGE 05 — WHY THIS STORY

### Being remembered is not the same as being known.

The Golden Record is an extraordinary act of optimism: a civilisation introducing itself to strangers it may never meet. But a record can only preserve what someone chose to put on it.

*The Last Boy on Earth* begins with that beautiful limitation.

The visitors arrive knowing humanity through an artefact. The boy knows humanity through absence, habits, rooms, recordings, mistakes and the stubborn traces of ordinary lives. Neither version is complete.

The series is about what happens when an archive meets a witness.

Under the science-fiction mystery is a simple emotional idea for children and adults: we do not become real to one another by collecting information. We become real through attention, trust and the willingness to listen when the answer complicates the story we expected to hear.

**Image assignment**  
Intimate detail: the Golden Record or its image reflected in the boy’s eye, with an alien hand entering frame but not touching him. Gold is the only heightened colour cue.

---

## PAGE 06 — THE EMOTIONAL PROMISE

### A mystery with wonder in its bones.

This is not a story about wandering through a dead world. Earth is alive. The comedy comes from first contact, the suspense from what has been hidden, and the emotion from two young people discovering that each possesses a piece of the world the other cannot reach alone.

**WONDER**  
Seeing Earth as extraordinary because a visitor has never seen rain, birds, kitchens, traffic lights or a human bedroom.

**LONELINESS**  
The quiet truth beneath the adventure: one child has had no equal companion.

**HUMOUR**  
Confident alien interpretations of completely ordinary human behaviour, and the boy’s disbelief that anyone could misunderstand it so badly.

**SUSPENSE**  
Signals, sealed orders and waking infrastructure steadily reveal that humanity’s absence is not the end of the story.

**BELONGING**  
Friendship grows through permission, mistakes, teaching and repair rather than instant sentimentality.

**HOPE**  
The season moves from an old message sent into silence toward a new message capable of receiving an answer.

**Closing line**  
The first Golden Record said: *This is who we were.*  
The new one asks: *Who are we to each other now?*

**Image assignment**  
The two children beneath a vast night sky sharing one listening device. Keep them small. The image should carry warmth without becoming sentimental or magical.
