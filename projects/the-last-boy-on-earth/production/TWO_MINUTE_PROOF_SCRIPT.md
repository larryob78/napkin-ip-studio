# THE LAST BOY ON EARTH
## Two-Minute Cinematic Proof — Script + Numbered Shot List v0.1

**Status:** Production-development draft  
**Target duration:** 2:00  
**Format:** Premium episodic animated science-fiction adventure  
**Purpose:** Prove premise, relationship, visual identity, performance, sound and continuity before scaling to episodic production.  
**Source authority:** `CANON.md` first; `GREENLIGHT_BOOK.md` is the canonical editorial source.  

## Canon boundary

This proof deliberately does **not** reveal or lock:

- the boy's final name or exact age
- his full biography or survival mechanism
- the cause of humanity's disappearance
- the Memory Ark mythology
- off-world human settlements
- the identity of any approaching force
- final alien personal names or institutional structure

The proof only needs to establish the locked premise: an alien expedition follows humanity's Golden Record to a breathtaking Earth reclaimed by nature, finds one surviving boy, and the beginning of friendship opens a larger mystery.

---

# Creative objective

In two minutes the audience should feel four things in sequence:

**AWE → UNEASE → HUMOUR → CONNECTION → QUESTION**

The proof is not a trailer and not a compressed plot summary. It is one continuous dramatic encounter built around listening.

The visual rule is cinematic realism and tactile physicality inside premium animation. The camera behaves as though photographing a real place with real lenses, weather, weight and imperfect surfaces.

# SCRIPT

## 00:00–00:12 — DARK / THE INVITATION

Black.

Not silence. A faint bed of solar noise, low mechanical resonance and the soft physical crackle of an old recording medium.

A gold circle turns slowly through darkness.

Not glamorous. Scratched. Small. Human-made.

A fragment of a human greeting appears through static. It is incomplete enough to feel discovered rather than presented.

Cut wider.

The Golden Record becomes almost invisible against the scale of space.

A distant shape crosses frame.

The alien expedition has found it.

**No exposition. No title card yet.**

---

## 00:12–00:28 — ARRIVAL

Cloud fills frame.

An asymmetrical alien craft moves through wet Atlantic weather. Its listening surfaces alter orientation as it descends. Nothing about it reads as attack posture.

Through a break in cloud: Earth.

Green. Wet. Alive.

Dublin appears below, transformed into an ecosystem. The Liffey threads through vegetation and old civic geometry. Towers carry trees. Roads have become water routes.

There are no visible human lights.

The ship passes silently over the city.

On a high roof, a small figure sees it.

The boy freezes.

Then disappears from view.

---

## 00:28–00:46 — THE BOY

Ground level.

Child-height camera moving carefully through a flooded transit concourse.

Water reacts to every footstep. Roots hang through fractured ceiling panels. Old signs remain legible beneath mineral staining.

The alien young scientist enters frame, holding a tactile field recorder.

A tiny sound behind them.

They turn.

Nothing.

A second crew member points toward a recently disturbed line in the shallow water.

The scanner is suddenly gone from the scientist's hand.

Across the concourse, the boy stands half-hidden behind a column holding it.

First eye contact.

Nobody moves.

---

## 00:46–01:04 — FIRST CONTACT / MISUNDERSTANDING

The scientist lowers both hands and slowly places a small object on the ground: an alien memory token.

The boy watches.

Beside him lies an abandoned human umbrella, half-open and tangled in roots.

The scientist studies it, then carefully rotates the field recorder around it as though documenting an important ceremonial instrument.

The boy's guarded expression breaks for half a second.

He points at the umbrella.

Then at the rain falling through the broken roof.

He opens it.

A beat.

The scientist looks up at the hole in the roof, then back at the umbrella.

Understanding.

The boy almost laughs.

The ship intelligence quietly emits an alert tone.

The scientist makes the alien gesture for correction or acknowledgement.

The boy has no idea what it means.

Now the scientist almost laughs.

---

## 01:04–01:25 — THE RECORD

A soft melodic phrase begins from the scientist's device.

The boy changes instantly.

He knows it.

The humour leaves without the warmth disappearing.

He steps closer for the first time.

The scientist notices his recognition and stops recording.

They offer the device rather than pointing it at him.

The boy hesitates.

Takes it.

Close on two different hands sharing the same object.

The music continues.

**Music note:** use a legally cleared excerpt connected to the Voyager Golden Record only if rights are confirmed. Until then, use an original temporary cue that carries the dramatic function without imitating a protected recording.

---

## 01:25–01:42 — TRUST

The boy reaches into his repaired bag.

The security officer shifts weight, ready but not aggressive.

The boy sees the movement.

Stops.

The scientist turns one open palm toward the security officer: stay.

A beat.

The boy removes a small human playback device, heavily repaired and clearly old.

**PROVISIONAL PROP:** this supports the current recommended character dossier but does not lock the final signature object.

He presses play.

The same melodic phrase emerges through damaged human speakers, thinner and older.

The scientist looks from the device to the boy.

This is the first moment they understand he is not simply surviving among ruins. He has been listening.

---

## 01:42–01:55 — THE ANSWER

A new sound interrupts both devices.

Three low pulses.

Pause.

Three low pulses again.

The boy's face changes before the aliens understand why.

He looks toward the dark tunnel beyond the concourse.

The scientist's recorder traces a physical amber path across its surface.

The signal is local.

Recent.

The boy knows this sound too.

He gives the alien device back.

Then, for the first time, gestures for them to follow.

---

## 01:55–02:00 — TITLE

Wide from behind.

The boy leads the young scientist toward the dark tunnel. The rest of the crew remains a few steps back.

The frame holds the distance between strangers becoming a group.

Cut to black.

# THE LAST BOY ON EARTH

**Optional sub-line:**  
*They crossed the stars to meet humanity. They arrived too late.*

One final signal pulse.

End.

# Dialogue strategy

The proof should work with almost no translated dialogue. That prevents premature locking of alien voice, language and names while making the film internationally legible.

Permitted sound elements:

- fragments of a historical human greeting, subject to source/rights clearance
- breath and small vocal reactions
- alien vocal texture without subtitled semantic content
- practical environmental sound
- one musical bridge
- the three-pulse mystery signal

If a spoken line is added during performance testing, it should be no more than one short human phrase from the boy and should not expose biography or mythology.

---

# Sound design spine

## 1. Space
Near-silence, solar texture, record-surface crackle.

## 2. Earth
Wind, rain, water, birds, building movement. No generic sci-fi ambience laid over Dublin.

## 3. Alien technology
Tactile pressure, material resonance, localised sound. Avoid electronic beeps as default language.

## 4. Human music
The emotional centre. When it begins, the environmental mix creates room around it.

## 5. Signal
Three low pulses that are simple enough to recognise again in later development.

# Production assumptions to test

The proof should answer practical production questions, not only look attractive.

### Character
- Can the same boy and alien faces survive wide, medium and 85mm close framing?
- Can eyes, fingers, cloth and subtle expressions hold up under intimate shots?
- Can the scientist read as warm and unmistakably non-human without becoming cute or mascot-like?

### Environment
- Can reclaimed Dublin remain geographically and ecologically coherent across aerial and interior scales?
- Can water, rain, roots and cloth interact without synthetic artefacts?

### Camera
- Can restrained camera motion preserve believable lens behaviour and spatial continuity?
- Can child-height framing become a repeatable series language?

### Sound
- Can human music carry the emotional bridge without explanatory dialogue?
- Can alien sound feel culturally specific instead of generic electronic UI?

### Editing
- Does the sequence move clearly from awe to caution to humour to trust to mystery in two minutes?

---

# Approval gates

The proof is greenlit for final finishing only when:

- boy identity is visually consistent across all shots
- alien anatomy and costume remain consistent
- first-contact eye-lines work physically
- humour comes from character misunderstanding, not slapstick
- the boy makes an active choice by the ending
- Earth feels alive rather than post-apocalyptic
- the Golden Record is emotionally legible without exposition
- the final signal expands the mystery without explaining it
- no provisional mythology has been presented as locked fact
- sound works even before final dialogue
- the sequence looks like one film, not eighteen unrelated generated clips
