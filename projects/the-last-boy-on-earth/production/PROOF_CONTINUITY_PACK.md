# THE LAST BOY ON EARTH
## Two-Minute Proof Continuity Pack v0.1

**Status:** Production-ready visual development specification  
**Purpose:** Lock the minimum reusable visual references required to storyboard and generate the 120-second Tencent proof as one coherent film.  
**Source hierarchy:** `CANON.md` → `GREENLIGHT_BOOK.md` → `CONTINUITY_REVIEW.md` → character/world/design bibles → this production specification.  
**Related production source:** `production/TWO_MINUTE_PROOF_SCRIPT.md`

---

# 1. What this pack is for

The two-minute proof contains only eighteen shots, but those shots cannot be generated as eighteen independent images or clips. They must inherit the same faces, proportions, wardrobe, props, scale, materials, environment rules and spatial relationships.

This pack therefore defines fourteen approval references that become the **visual source of truth for the proof**.

Once a reference is approved, later storyboard frames and motion generations must inherit from it rather than reinterpret it.

The goal is not to create fourteen beautiful concept images. The goal is to create fourteen dependable production references that make eighteen shots look as though they belong to one film.

---

# 2. Canon boundary

Approval of this pack does **not** automatically lock story mythology.

The following remain unresolved unless separately approved in `CANON.md`:

- the boy's final name
- the boy's exact age
- his full family or carer history
- exact duration of his isolation
- the final signature personal object
- the exact cause of humanity's disappearance
- the Memory Ark as final mythology
- off-world human communities as final canon
- final alien personal names
- final alien institutional structure
- final identity of any approaching force

Visual references may be approved for production use while still carrying **PROVISIONAL** labels for unresolved story details.

# 3. Global visual standard

## Format

Premium episodic animation with photographic credibility.

The animation should behave as though photographed in a real physical world:

- believable lenses and depth of field
- physically coherent weather
- real material weight
- tactile skin, cloth, metal, water and glass
- restrained camera behaviour
- optical imperfection rather than synthetic sharpness
- natural asymmetry

This is not a live-action format change. Photographic realism is the craft benchmark for animation.

## Earth

Earth is ecologically alive and visually beautiful. Avoid generic apocalypse shorthand.

Required recurring qualities:

- wet Atlantic atmosphere
- real Dublin civic geometry
- nature structurally transforming architecture
- uneven weathering
- reflective water
- ordinary traces of human life
- silence carried by wind, rain, birds and old structures

## Alien design

Alien characters must read as one coherent civilisation without relying on familiar grey-alien shorthand.

Core development grammar:

- expressive but non-human anatomy
- tactile layered clothing
- visible repair and use
- culturally coherent tools
- quiet mineral materials
- no neon cyberpunk language
- no decorative floating holograms

## Colour

Primary world range:

- wet greens
- slate blue
- pale stone
- aged brick
- mineral grey
- deep desaturated blue

Warm amber and gold are reserved for memory, reply, invitation and moral choice.

# 4. Reference approval states

Each item moves through four states:

**WIP** — exploratory; not reusable as continuity authority.  
**REVIEW** — candidate reference; may be compared but not propagated.  
**APPROVED** — locked for the proof and reusable downstream.  
**REJECTED** — retained only for learning; must never seed later assets.

## Naming convention

`LBOE_PROOF_REF##_SHORT-DESCRIPTOR_v##_STATUS.ext`

Example:

`LBOE_PROOF_REF01_BOY-PORTRAIT_v04_APPROVED.png`

Every approved image must also record:

- source prompt or brief version
- approval date
- approving creative owner
- inherited references
- known limitations
- shots that depend on it

## REF 01 — BOY FRONT PORTRAIT

**Priority:** CRITICAL  
**Canon status:** identity details provisional; emotional and visual function locked  
**Proof shots:** 07, 09, 10, 11, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18

### Production job

Establish the definitive face used by every later proof image and shot.

### Required frame

85mm-equivalent animated portrait at child eye level. Head and upper torso. Neutral reclaimed civic interior or soft exterior background with enough environmental cue to belong to the Earth world without competing with the face.

### Character read

The boy must look:

- observant before expressive
- competent without looking hardened into an action hero
- wary of people rather than frightened of the world
- emotionally readable at very small changes of expression
- still recognisably a child

### Wardrobe visible in frame

- repaired weatherproof outer layer
- softer older inner garment
- practical strap or field bag element
- repairs that imply time rather than stylised distressing

### Avoid

- heroic jaw-up pose
- dystopian grime as personality
- tactical costume
- fantasy chosen-one styling
- overtly fixed ethnicity, exact age or biographical symbolism unless separately approved
- exaggerated sadness

### Approval test

The portrait passes only if the same face can plausibly support:

1. guarded eye contact
2. suppressed amusement
3. recognition of music
4. alarm at the signal
5. the final decision to trust

### Downstream lock

On approval, freeze:

- facial proportions
- eye colour and shape
- hairline and hair length
- skin texture family
- ear shape
- nose and mouth geometry
- visible freckles/scars only if deliberately approved

## REF 02 — BOY FULL BODY + REPAIRED WARDROBE

**Priority:** CRITICAL  
**Canon status:** wardrobe logic approved for production; exact signature object remains provisional  
**Proof shots:** 04, 07, 09, 14, 17, 18

### Production job

Lock silhouette, body proportions, wardrobe layers, carried bag and footwear.

### Required plate

Front, three-quarter and side full-body on one neutral reference field, plus one practical walking pose.

### Silhouette rules

- compact child proportions
- no superhero stance
- repaired clothing built from practical need
- one recognisable bag/strap shape
- footwear credible for wet surfaces and climbing
- silhouette readable from 20–30 metres in a wide frame

### Wardrobe logic

Every layer must answer one question: why does he still have it?

Preferred system:

- waterproof outer shell with visible repairs
- warm mid-layer with domestic history
- practical trousers modified at knees/pockets
- weathered but maintained shoes or boots
- field bag containing small tools and recording equipment

### Approval test

The figure must remain recognisable:

- from behind
- in rain
- in silhouette
- beside alien characters
- when only one colour accent is visible

## REF 03 — YOUNG SCIENTIST FRONT / THREE-QUARTER PORTRAIT

**Priority:** CRITICAL  
**Canon status:** species anatomy provisional until approved; dramatic role locked  
**Proof shots:** 05, 07, 08, 09, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 16, 17, 18

### Production job

Create the proof's second emotional anchor: unmistakably alien, fully performable, capable of warmth without becoming cute.

### Required frame

Matched front and three-quarter 85mm portraits with identical lighting and costume state.

### Required read

- intense curiosity
- emotional directness
- intelligence before innocence
- non-human anatomy capable of subtle eye-lines and micro-expression
- open attentiveness rather than mascot energy

### Current design grammar to test

- subtly widened upper skull
- large but not childish eyes
- fine secondary eye membrane
- quiet mineral skin quality
- narrow flexible sound-sensitive ear structures
- forward-balanced attentive posture

All remain provisional until this reference is approved.

### Avoid

- familiar grey alien
- human actor with decorative prosthetics
- blue skin as the primary alien signal
- oversized eyes designed for cuteness
- glossy amphibian skin
- fantasy horns or ornamental bioluminescence

### Approval test

The scientist must be capable of showing, with tiny changes:

1. scientific concentration
2. being wrong about the umbrella
3. embarrassment
4. shared humour
5. recognition that the boy knows the music
6. concern when the final signal appears

## REF 04 — SCIENTIST FULL-BODY SILHOUETTE + FIELD RECORDER

**Priority:** CRITICAL  
**Canon status:** role and tool function development-approved; exact anatomy provisional  
**Proof shots:** 05, 08, 09, 12, 14, 16, 17, 18

### Production job

Lock the scientist's silhouette, field clothing and primary tactile recorder as one coherent design system.

### Required plate

- front full body
- profile
- three-quarter walking stance
- recorder closed
- recorder active
- recorder being offered to a human hand

### Silhouette

- lighter and more asymmetrical than the leader/security roles
- accessible equipment rather than armour
- quick but not nervous posture
- physical ability to lower toward child eye line

### Field recorder rules

The recorder is palm-sized and layered.

It must:

- look handled and repaired
- reveal function physically
- contain no generic floating screen
- carry light through channels in the material
- support pressure/orientation interaction
- be safe to hand from alien to child

### Proof-specific active state

For Shot 16 the recorder must create a physical amber trace across its surface when the local signal appears.

### Approval test

Recorder scale and grip must work in both alien and human hands without changing size.

---

## REF 05 — SECURITY OFFICER SILHOUETTE + PROTECTIVE TOOL

**Priority:** HIGH  
**Canon status:** character role locked; exact design provisional  
**Proof shots:** 06, 14, 18

### Production job

Create a presence that can read as intimidating at first glance while remaining capable of protective gentleness.

### Silhouette

- broadest crew silhouette
- width created by protective equipment, not monstrous anatomy
- compact tools close to body
- planted stance
- strong lateral movement

### Protective tool

No conventional gun design.

Current functional brief:

- maps structural risk
- can anchor unstable material
- can create a brief defensive barrier
- can restrain without injury

Its visual grammar must belong to the same civilisation as the scientist's recorder.

### Proof-specific behaviour

In Shot 14 the officer shifts weight when the boy reaches into his bag. The movement must read as trained caution, not threat escalation.

### Approval test

A still frame with the officer relaxed should feel safe. The same design under tension should feel formidable without changing costume or adding weapons.

## REF 06 — CREW GROUP SCALE PLATE

**Priority:** CRITICAL  
**Canon status:** individual names unresolved; relative physical relationships production-locked once approved  
**Proof shots:** 03, 04, 05, 14, 18

### Production job

Lock relative height, shoulder level, stride, hand scale and eye-line geometry across the proof ensemble.

### Required plate

Neutral side-by-side lineup containing:

- boy
- young scientist
- security officer
- expedition leader if present in the proof continuity world

Include front and side measuring views.

### Required measurements

Record relative rather than absolute numbers if exact ages/anatomy remain provisional.

Example structure:

- boy eye line = baseline 1.00
- scientist eye line = X relative to boy
- security eye line = X relative to boy
- hand length ratios
- shoulder width ratios
- standing reach ratios

### Approval test

The first-contact composition must be achievable without cheating character scale from shot to shot.

## REF 07 — SHIP EXTERIOR / ATMOSPHERIC CONFIGURATION

**Priority:** CRITICAL  
**Canon status:** ship cultural function developed; final form provisional until visual approval  
**Proof shots:** 02, 03, 04

### Production job

Lock the craft seen discovering the Record and descending through Atlantic cloud.

### Core metaphor

The ship is a **travelling instrument for listening**.

### Exterior rules

- asymmetrical but balanced
- no saucer silhouette
- no front-facing fighter cockpit
- layered receiving/listening surfaces
- inhabited core protected inside the larger structure
- visible repair and mission history
- atmospheric configuration may fold or reorient surfaces physically
- movement should feel measured, not aggressive

### Required views

- deep-space silhouette
- three-quarter orbit view
- atmospheric descent configuration
- underside scale view
- one material close-up

### Weather test

The approved ship must remain readable when partially obscured by dense cloud and rain.

### Approval test

If the craft is shown with no characters, the viewer should infer exploration/listening before warfare.

## REF 08 — FLOODED TRANSIT CONCOURSE MASTER ENVIRONMENT

**Priority:** CRITICAL  
**Canon status:** environment family locked; exact real-world site may remain production-selectable  
**Proof shots:** 05–18

### Production job

Create the one location capable of supporting nearly the entire dramatic encounter without spatial contradictions.

### Required master environment

A recognisably Dublin transit/civic interior with:

- shallow standing water
- fractured roof or ceiling allowing rain entry
- roots descending through structural openings
- old human signage
- at least three large columns or spatial dividers
- dark tunnel/deeper route for the final signal
- enough open area for first contact
- one area of denser roots containing the abandoned umbrella

### Spatial zones

Define and number:

A. crew entry  
B. water disturbance  
C. boy reveal column  
D. memory-token placement zone  
E. umbrella position  
F. shared-listening position  
G. tunnel/signal direction  
H. final exit path

### Required production outputs

- master wide
- overhead floor plan
- child-height reverse angle
- tunnel-facing angle
- material close-ups: water, stone, roots, signage

### Environmental logic

Water depth must remain consistent. Rainfall must have a plausible source. Roots must follow light/water. Signage must remain fixed. Columns cannot move between shots.

### Approval test

Every proof shot from 05 through 18 must be placeable on the same simple floor plan.

## REF 09 — GOLDEN RECORD MATERIAL + SCALE REFERENCE

**Priority:** CRITICAL  
**Canon status:** historical object as story inspiration locked; rights/use details require legal/production clearance  
**Proof shots:** 01, 02, 11–13 by sonic/thematic association

### Production job

Create the physical reference used for macro opening imagery and all later Record-related visuals.

### Required qualities

- finite physical object
- warm metallic surface
- fine scratches and evidence of age/use appropriate to the story treatment
- etched information treated with respect and accuracy where reproduced
- no magical glow
- gold only brightens through motivated reflected light

### Required plate

- face-on scale view
- edge thickness
- macro scratch/material sample
- rotating three-quarter lighting study

### Accuracy rule

If the actual Voyager Golden Record design is reproduced in final commercial material, confirm image/design usage, factual markings and any licensing or rights considerations before release.

### Approval test

The object must feel emotionally precious because it is handmade and small, not because visual effects make it supernatural.

## REF 10 — HUMAN UMBRELLA PROP

**Priority:** MEDIUM / STORY-CRITICAL  
**Canon status:** proof-specific prop; does not alter wider canon  
**Proof shots:** 09

### Production job

Support the proof's main comic misunderstanding through physical credibility.

### Required prop

A recognisably ordinary human umbrella:

- aged
- partly trapped in roots
- mechanically understandable
- still capable of opening
- not visually iconic or branded

### Continuity requirements

Define:

- canopy colour/material
- handle shape
- number and shape of visible damaged ribs
- open/closed state before the boy touches it
- precise root entanglement

### Approval test

The joke must work silently: an audience should understand what the umbrella does before the scientist does.

## REF 11 — ALIEN MEMORY TOKEN

**Priority:** HIGH  
**Canon status:** cultural object provisional but compatible with existing alien design grammar  
**Proof shots:** 08–10

### Production job

Provide the scientist's first nonverbal act of trust.

### Object function

The token is a small personal memory object, not currency and not a gadget introduced to solve plot.

It should imply:

- prior ownership
- tactile use
- emotional or archival meaning
- cultural continuity with the field recorder

### Required plate

- palm scale
- top/side/underside
- inactive state
- subtle active memory state if needed
- placed on wet floor beside alien hand for scale

### Design rule

No floating display, jewel-like fantasy glow or obvious human iconography.

### Approval test

When the scientist places it down, the gesture should read as giving up something personal rather than deploying equipment.

## REF 12 — HUMAN PLAYBACK DEVICE (PROVISIONAL)

**Priority:** HIGH  
**Canon status:** **PROVISIONAL PROP — NOT FINAL CHARACTER LOCK**  
**Proof shots:** 14, 15

### Production job

Give the boy a believable way to reveal that he has been listening to the same music before the visitors arrived.

### Current recommended form

Small repaired pocket audio playback/recording device with:

- several generations of repair
- tactile mechanical controls
- mismatched replacement material
- small speaker
- no pristine screen
- no unexplained supercomputer capability

### Story constraint

The prop must do only what the proof requires: play stored audio.

It must not silently become the final solution to his biography, a map, a key or an exposition machine.

### Required plate

- front/back/edge
- in boy's hand
- clipped/stored in bag
- playing state
- repair detail macro

### Approval label

Even if visually approved for the proof, filename metadata must preserve `PROVISIONAL-PROP` until founder lock.

## REF 13 — HAND-CONTACT SCALE SHEET

**Priority:** CRITICAL  
**Canon status:** anatomical design provisional until approved; scale becomes proof-locked  
**Proof shots:** 08, 12, 13, 14, 17

### Production job

Prevent the most visible continuity failure in first-contact animation: hands changing anatomy, size or contact geometry from shot to shot.

### Required sheet

Matched orthographic and interaction studies for:

- boy hand open / gripping recorder
- scientist hand open / gripping recorder
- scientist hand offering recorder
- both hands on recorder simultaneously
- scientist acknowledgement gesture
- security officer relaxed hand silhouette for comparison

### Alien hand grammar to test

Current provisional concept:

- four long fingers
- one stabilising inner digit
- precise tactile movement

This remains provisional until approved.

### Contact physics

Recorder dimensions must be fixed before final hand-contact approval.

Check:

- finger wrap
- pressure points
- hand overlap
- object centre of gravity
- cloth interaction at wrists

### Approval test

Shot 12 should be producible without hiding fingers, cheating scale or cutting away from contact.

## REF 14 — EYE-LINE + HEIGHT CHART

**Priority:** CRITICAL  
**Canon status:** relative staging production-locked once approved  
**Proof shots:** 07, 09, 10, 11, 13, 14, 17, 18

### Production job

Lock the physical relationship that makes first contact emotionally believable.

### Required chart

Show boy, scientist and security officer in:

- neutral standing positions
- boy ↔ scientist direct eye contact
- scientist lowered to boy eye level
- security officer observing from behind
- seated/crouched equivalent if used

### Camera overlays

Add:

- child-height 35mm camera line
- 50mm shared two-shot line
- 85mm reciprocal close-up lines

### Staging principle

The scientist earns intimacy partly by changing physical height and distance. The security officer initially remains further back. These distances should reduce through the proof rather than reset randomly.

### Approval test

Shots 07 and 18 should clearly show a spatial relationship change even with sound removed.

# 9. Review procedure

Every candidate reference should be reviewed in this order:

## A. Canon

Does it introduce an unapproved story fact?

If yes, reject or relabel the fact as provisional before approval.

## B. Character

Does the design support the character's dramatic role and emotional range?

## C. Physical continuity

Can it coexist at believable scale with already approved references?

## D. Material reality

Do skin, cloth, metal, water and props show real weight, contact and wear?

## E. Camera survivability

Will the design still hold up under wide, 50mm and 85mm-equivalent framing?

## F. Motion survivability

Can the design move without anatomy or garment logic breaking?

## G. Anti-cliché test

Is the solution specific to this series rather than generic science-fiction concept art?

---

# 10. Rejection triggers

Reject a candidate even if it is visually impressive when any of the following appear:

- boy identity drifts from previously approved reference
- alien anatomy changes between views
- scientist becomes cute/mascot-like
- security officer becomes militarised
- alien technology defaults to blue floating holograms
- ship reads primarily as a weapon
- Dublin becomes generic ruined megacity
- vegetation is decorative rather than structural
- water geometry changes impossibly
- prop scale changes between hands
- hands are hidden to avoid anatomy problems
- eye-lines require impossible character heights
- warm gold is used everywhere rather than reserved for memory/reply
- provisional mythology is presented as factual design explanation
- the asset looks more like AI concept art than a frame from one coherent animated film

# 13. What approval means

Approving a reference means:

- it becomes the default visual seed for downstream production
- later assets must inherit its relevant geometry/materials
- deviations require an explicit version change
- rejected alternatives remain excluded
- unresolved story facts remain unresolved unless separately changed in `CANON.md`

The pack is complete when all fourteen references are either APPROVED or deliberately replaced by a documented alternative.
