# Napkin IP Studio Skill

## Purpose

Develop a premium episodic series from a raw concept into a coherent, visually ownable and production-ready IP package.

## Required inputs

- Working title
- Format and episode length
- Genre
- Core premise
- Human truth
- Intended audience
- Known creative constraints
- Commercial or partner requirements

## Required outputs

1. Story Bible
2. Character Bible
3. World Bible
4. Season Arc
5. Ten Episode Treatments
6. Brand Bible
7. Art Direction Bible
8. Production Bible
9. Two-Minute Test Brief
10. Executive Pitch Summary

## Non-negotiable rules

- Read the project's CANON.md before every task.
- Do not contradict locked decisions.
- Mark new assumptions clearly.
- Never confuse visual reference with story tone.
- Character decisions must create plot consequences.
- Each episode must have a beginning, escalation, emotional turn and ending hook.
- Every episode must alter a relationship, reveal character or advance mythology.
- Avoid generic AI language, filler and repetitive summaries.
- Preserve international clarity without sanding away originality.
- Copyright and confidentiality notices must appear in distributable documents.

## Story method

Use classical cause-and-effect storytelling and character-driven animation principles:

1. Begin with an emotionally recognisable truth.
2. Define what the protagonist wants.
3. Define what the protagonist actually needs.
4. Give the protagonist a flaw that creates problems.
5. Make choices, not coincidence, drive escalation.
6. Test the central relationship repeatedly.
7. Build an ending that feels surprising but inevitable.
8. Leave the audience with a changed character and a renewed story engine.

## Episodic method

Each 15-minute episode should contain:

- Episode question
- Emotional truth
- A story
- Optional B story
- Inciting incident
- Progressive complications
- Midpoint turn
- Crisis or lowest point
- Character choice
- Climax
- Emotional resolution
- Season-arc movement
- Final hook

## Character method

Every principal character requires:

- Role in the series
- External goal
- Internal need
- Core flaw
- Fear
- Emotional wound
- Contradiction
- Signature behaviour
- Relationship map
- Season arc
- Visual silhouette
- Voice and dialogue pattern
- Story engine

## Visual-development method

Define:

- Visual thesis
- Shape language
- Character design rules
- Environment rules
- Colour system
- Lighting
- Materials
- Camera and lens language
- Composition
- Motion principles
- VFX language
- Typography
- Graphic identity
- Image-generation guardrails
- What the project must never look like

## Brand-development method

Define:

- Brand promise
- Audience
- Emotional territory
- Values
- Tone of voice
- Ownable symbols
- Taglines
- Social behaviour
- Franchise opportunities
- Merchandise principles
- Licensing guardrails

## Review gates

A section cannot be locked until it scores at least 8/10 for:

- Emotional truth
- Character depth
- Originality
- Clarity
- Episodic momentum
- Visual potential
- Continuity
- International appeal
- Production feasibility
- Franchise potential

## Completion condition

The project is Version 1 complete only when all required outputs exist, all continuity risks are resolved or logged, every episode has a distinct purpose, and the two-minute proof-of-concept brief represents the series accurately.
