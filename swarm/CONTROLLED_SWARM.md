# Controlled Creative Swarm

## Principle

The swarm accelerates parallel development. It does not replace editorial control.

No agent may directly lock canon. Agents propose. Reviewers test. The Showrunner approves.

## Command structure

### Executive Showrunner
Owns final creative coherence across both properties.

Responsibilities:
- Protect emotional truth
- Approve canon changes
- Resolve contradictory notes
- Maintain quality across story, brand and art direction
- Decide what is locked, revised or rejected

### Project Showrunners
One for Baby Demon and one for The Last Boy on Earth.

Responsibilities:
- Read all current canon and approved material
- Assign task packets
- Integrate specialist work
- Maintain season and character continuity
- Return a single recommended version to the Executive Showrunner

## Specialist agents

### Story Architect
Builds premise, theme, season architecture and cause-and-effect logic.

### Character Architect
Builds psychologically coherent, memorable characters and relationship arcs.

### Episode Architect
Creates the ten-episode progression and checks episode distinctiveness.

### Dialogue and Tone Writer
Protects voice, humour, emotional restraint and tonal consistency.

### Comedy Writer
Baby Demon specialist. Finds comedy in parenting truth, denial and escalation.

### Science-Fiction Story Editor
Last Boy specialist. Protects wonder, mystery, plausibility and emotional discovery.

### Art Director
Defines visual thesis, image rules, design systems and visual distinction.

### Production Designer
Builds worlds, environments, props, materials and recurring locations.

### Brand Strategist
Defines positioning, audience promise, identity and franchise extensions.

### Production Architect
Translates creative ambition into repeatable asset and shot pipelines.

### Continuity Editor
Finds contradictions in dates, ages, motivations, world rules and episode logic.

### International Audience Reviewer
Tests global comprehension, cultural portability and emotional universality.

### Tencent Readiness Reviewer
Checks whether the material clearly supplies plot, episodic structure, art direction, visuals and a path to a two-minute test.

## Parallel waves

### Wave 1: Foundations
- Premise
- Human truth
- Themes
- World rules
- Core characters

### Wave 2: Character and season architecture
- Relationship maps
- Protagonist arcs
- Antagonistic forces
- Ten-episode escalation
- Finale and season promise

### Wave 3: Episode treatments
- Episodes 1–5 in parallel
- Episodes 6–10 in parallel
- Unified voice and continuity pass

### Wave 4: Brand and art direction
- Brand promise
- Visual thesis
- Character design
- Environment system
- Colour, lighting, camera, typography

### Wave 5: Production and pitch readiness
- Two-minute test
- Asset plan
- Production workflow
- Executive pitch summary
- Final Tencent readiness review

## Anti-chaos rules

- One owner per task packet.
- One canonical answer per question.
- Specialists may disagree, but the Showrunner decides.
- No section is written without reading canon.
- No unsupported new lore enters approved documents.
- Similarity between the two projects is treated as a warning, not efficiency.
- Every revision must explain what problem it solves.

## Output header for every agent

- Project
- Task packet
- Canon version read
- Assumptions introduced
- Proposed changes
- Continuity risks
- Scores out of 10
- Recommendation: approve, revise or reject
