# THE LAST BOY ON EARTH — TENCENT DECK SWARM

**Branch:** `lboe-development-sprint`  
**Objective:** Produce a coherent, premium 10-page Tencent development deck from approved and provisional repository material.  
**Editorial owner:** Executive Showrunner. No specialist output enters the final deck without integration and continuity review.

## Final deck structure

1. Cover — title, tagline, hero image direction
2. The hook — logline and two-paragraph series synopsis
3. Why audiences care — emotional promise, genre, format and U.S. positioning
4. The world — reclaimed Earth, Golden Record and central mystery
5. The characters — Eli, Tavi, Sela, Orro and ARO
6. Season One — complete dramatic arc in concise prose
7. Episode guide — ten episode hooks
8. Visual language — cinematic rules and twelve-image proof plan
9. Why Napkin — canon-controlled AI-native development and production pathway
10. Next step — four-week Tencent StoryLab and two-minute proof

## Agent assignments

### 1. Executive Showrunner
**Owns:** Final voice, canon safety and deck coherence.  
**Reads:** CANON.md, Development Locks, Season Map, Character Bible, Tencent Pitch Document.  
**Delivers:** One integrated final manuscript; approves or rejects all specialist proposals.

### 2. Science-Fiction Story Editor
**Owns:** Series synopsis, mystery clarity and emotional restraint.  
**Delivers:** A two-paragraph premium synopsis and a one-page Season One narrative.  
**Quality test:** Wonder before action; mythology always changes a relationship.

### 3. Character Architect
**Owns:** Eli and expedition character clarity.  
**Delivers:** Five concise character profiles, each with want, wound, contradiction and season turn.  
**Quality test:** Every character is memorable in two sentences and visually distinct.

### 4. Episode Architect
**Owns:** Ten-episode binge progression.  
**Delivers:** Ten one-line hooks plus a continuity and escalation check.  
**Quality test:** Every episode contains a distinct location, emotional turn, reveal and cliffhanger.

### 5. Development Executive
**Owns:** U.S. market clarity and commissioning logic.  
**Delivers:** 'Why this series now', audience promise, format rationale and franchise potential.  
**Quality test:** Commercial without sounding generic or trend-chasing.

### 6. Art Director
**Owns:** Visual thesis and deck image plan.  
**Delivers:** One visual north star, page-by-page image requirements and consistency gates.  
**Quality test:** Premium photoreal science fiction; no generic saucers, grey aliens or AI-demo look.

### 7. Production Architect
**Owns:** Two-minute proof, production route and indicative four-week StoryLab.  
**Delivers:** Clear workflow, deliverables, approval gates and producer handoff assumptions.  
**Quality test:** Credible, specific and careful not to promise unsupported cost or schedule certainty.

### 8. Tencent Readiness Reviewer
**Owns:** Final buyer-side critique.  
**Delivers:** Red-team notes against Tencent's brief: horizontal, premium, U.S.-focused, 10–20 minute episodes, genre-led and visually ambitious.  
**Quality test:** The deck creates one obvious next decision.

### 9. Continuity Editor
**Owns:** Canon, terminology and contradiction audit.  
**Delivers:** A locked/unlocked register covering Eli Byrne, Vael, Chorus, Quiet, Haven, crew names and finale choices.  
**Quality test:** No provisional concept is presented as approved fact without a label.

### 10. Pitch Editor
**Owns:** Compression and presentation rhythm.  
**Delivers:** Final 10-page copy with no page overloaded and no duplicated argument.  
**Quality test:** Story first, technology second, ask last.

### Wave D — Integration
- Continuity audit
- Tencent red-team review
- Executive Showrunner rewrite
- Final deck manuscript

## Non-negotiables

- The Golden Record remains the emotional and visual anchor.
- Eli's movement from isolation to belonging is the season spine.
- The Quiet cannot read as a generic alien invasion.
- AI production language appears only after the story has earned interest.
- The deck sells a joint development decision, not an unsupported promise of a finished series.
- Every page must answer a distinct buyer question.

## Definition of done

The sprint is complete when the repository contains:

- `TENCENT-DECK-MANUSCRIPT-v1.md`
- `TENCENT-DECK-IMAGE-PLAN-v1.md`
- `TENCENT-STORYLAB-SCOPE-v1.md`
- A canon and continuity audit
- A final ten-page editable deck generated from the approved manuscript
